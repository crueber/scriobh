# Phase 7: PDF import and tracked-changes import (F-374, F-378)

Backend and ops only. Section AR of [FEATURES](../FEATURES.md) has the rows; this file has the decisions, the limits, what was measured and what was not.

## What is built

- **`internal/pdfread`** (standard library only, no dependency added): a PDF reader written for files somebody else made. Objects, cross-reference tables and streams, hybrid files, object streams, a rebuild by scanning when the table is wrong, Flate, ASCII85, ASCIIHex, RunLength and LZW with PNG and TIFF predictors, page tree with inherited resources, content-stream text operators (including form XObjects and inline pictures, which are counted, never decoded), simple fonts (Standard, WinAnsi, MacRoman, Differences with Adobe glyph names, the encoding of an embedded Type 1 program), composite fonts (Identity, UCS2 and UTF16 CMaps, embedded CMaps), `ToUnicode` CMaps, widths, bold, italic and monospace from names and flags, outline with GoTo and named destinations. `pdfread/pdftest` assembles PDFs by hand for tests.
- **Layout** (`pdfread/layout.go`, `lines.go`, `assemble.go`): lines by baseline, two columns by gutter, running heads and page numbers, contents pages, footnotes, paragraphs, headings by size, lists, quotes, code, table rows, scene breaks, chapters from the outline or from headings, the fidelity report.
- **`internal/imports/pdfconv.go`**: the Markdown the other importers' folder path reads (through the Word converter's own renderer and composer, so escaping, footnote numbering and the tree are shared), the warnings, the report map. `pdf` is a field of `import.preview`, `import.apply`, `import.append_preview` and `import.append`; layout `pdf`; `project_sources.kind` `pdf` (already allowed by migration 1300).
- **Tracked changes and comments** (`imports/docxtrack.go`, changes in `docxconv.go`): `docx_changes` on the same four ops; `review.ImportSuggestionTx` and `review.ImportCommentTx` write them in the import's transaction through the review domain's own checks; migration `1820_review_author_labels.sql`; `docs.CheckEditsOnBody` (a pure form of the accept-time checks, so the preview and the apply decide from the same function).
- CLI: `scriobh import book.pdf`, `--docx-changes`.

## Decisions

**PDF**

1. **No OCR, no password.** A PDF with no text on most of its pages (fewer than half of the pages that are not blank carry text) is refused with a plain message. An encrypted PDF is refused whatever its password, including an owner-password-only file that would open with an empty user password: nothing is decrypted.
2. **Running heads rule** (stated in the report): a line in the top or bottom 8% of a page that stands apart from the text (more than 1.25 lines clear of its neighbour) and whose text, with digits read as one mark, is the same on at least 3 pages is a running head or foot and is removed. A line in those bands that is only a number, a roman numeral, "Page N" or "N of M" is a page number and is removed wherever it stands. Consequence, measured: a head that appears on fewer than 3 pages stays as text (the 12-page test book kept its verso author line once).
3. **Hyphens**: a soft hyphen at a line's end is removed. A hard hyphen is removed only when the letters on both sides are lower case, the stem has at least two letters and is not a prefix that is usually hyphenated (co, re, pre, non, anti, semi, self, ex, vice, half, all, ill, well, mid, post, pro, sub, ultra, multi, quasi, pseudo, cross, inter); otherwise it stays, with no space after it. The report counts both.
4. **Paragraphs**: a gap over 1.45 lines, a change of first-line indent, or (only in justified text, where the right edge is well defined) a short line that ends a sentence, begins a paragraph. A paragraph that runs over a page ends at the foot of one and begins at the head of the next (a line in the middle of a page does not continue the page before). A bookmark's target always begins a block.
5. **Chapters**: the outline when at least two bookmarks resolve (the shallowest level with two or more entries; a level whose entries own the next level's is parts; a top-level entry that owns none is a section of its own), otherwise headings by size class (a class that appears once on the first page is the title, parts when a class is followed at once by the next). The title page's heading and subtitle become the project's.
6. **Tables are flattened, not rebuilt**: three or more consecutive lines with cells set at the same place become one paragraph per row, cells separated by ` | `, and the page's confidence drops. A scene break is an ornament line, a lone `#`, `* * *`, or (counted separately) a gap over 2.4 lines in the middle of a page.
7. **Provenance** is the other importers': SHA-256 of the file, its name and size in `project_sources` (`kind` `pdf`, the report in `metadata_json`, bounded to the 100 least reliable pages with at most six notes each). There is no store of its own and none is read from the EPUB work.

**Tracked changes**

1. **The body holds the original** (every change rejected), each change inside a paragraph is a pending suggestion. Why: the author's text is the one fact of the file nobody chose to change; a reviewer's change is an editor's decision (AGENTS.md: nothing generated or proposed enters the text without an accept); and accepting every suggestion reproduces the reviewers' text, which a test proves against the `accept` import of the same file. `accept` (the behaviour before this release) and `reject` are options; the existing tests that assert the final view now ask for `accept`, deliberately.
2. **What cannot be a suggestion is taken as the reviewer left it, and counted** (the preview says so, with each kind): a whole paragraph inserted (text kept), a whole paragraph deleted (its text is **not kept**), a paragraph whose mark changed, a change in a heading, a table, a footnote or a code paragraph, a change over a cap, a change that is not found exactly once in the body, a change the review checks would refuse (including more than 50,000 bytes of new text), and a change at the edge of bold or italic text where proposing it would not give the final text. Last of these is a check, not a guess: for each paragraph the changes are applied to the original text and compared with the final text that was read, and the paragraph is taken as accepted when they differ (a mid-word insertion inside an italic run passes; one that crosses the boundary of the italics does not). A change that puts back the text that was there, or inserts nothing, is dropped without a count. The review checks refuse a replacement that starts a heading, list, quote or table the text did not have, carries markers, comments, control or direction characters, or a link the original lacked; paragraph breaks cannot be proposed. A change refused for any reason is applied to the text, never dropped: the two views are recomposed until what is left can all be placed.
3. **Changes that are close are one suggestion, of one author** (fix rounds 2 and 3). A suggestion is found again after another is accepted only by the 24 characters of context stored with it, exactly once, so two suggestions within a few words of each other cannot both be accepted one after the other, and `accept_all` treats an insertion at the start of a replaced range as an overlap. So: changes of one author that touch (an insertion beside a deletion) are one replacement; changes of one author whose contexts would overlap (closer than 52 characters, across paragraph breaks and list items too, because an insertion's context is not cut at a blank line) are one suggestion with several changes; a suggestion holds at most 50 changes and 50,000 bytes together, all checked in one pass. **A suggestion has one author**: changes of two authors that are that close cannot both be suggestions, and the label cannot be a joined name (round 2's "Ann and Bo" was cut at 100 characters, so thirty authors were shown as five). Of each such pair the later change (by the time Word recorded, else by place in the text) is taken as accepted: it is in the imported text, not a suggestion, and the preview counts it and names the two authors ("Bo / Ann"). **A visible consequence**: with two authors editing one sentence the imported body is not exactly the original, it holds the later author's change. Two changes of different authors whose ranges overlap are both taken as accepted. Last, each suggestion is checked with `review.PlaceableAfterOthers`, which asks the question the review screens ask: the first suggestion accepted is placed by its stored offsets, every later one by `alts.LocateIn` (a quote with its context, exactly once) or the insertion locator, so each change of a suggestion must be found, at its own place and nowhere else, in the original text and in every state reached by accepting some of the others. For up to five other suggestions every subset is tried; for more, the subsets of the nearby ones (within 400 bytes) and of the ones that contain its words, all of the others, and three fixed pseudo-random subsets: that case is sampled, not proven. A suggestion that fails is taken as accepted and counted, one at a time while there are few (two lines of a refrain are each unique only once the other is gone, and one of them can stay), and the rounds start again (up to 40; if they run out, what is left is taken as accepted rather than proposed unchecked). Touching changes are joined again after each round, since taking one as accepted makes its neighbours touch. The property test (`TestRandomTrackedChangeDocumentsAcceptToTheAcceptImport`: 240 random documents in four kinds, a small repetitive vocabulary, a large one, sparse changes in repeated text, and a five-word refrain, with one or three authors) accepts with `accept_all` and then one at a time in every order for up to six suggestions (a sample of four orders for more), and each result equals the `accept` import; it logs how many changes fell back, by which rule. The reviewer's own tests were changed where they encoded the old design: the repeated-text test (it required three suggestions; it now requires that what is written can be accepted in every order), the many-authors test (it now asserts one author for each suggestion and that the others are counted and named), the two `review_p7` tests (they now cover one author and two, near and far) and the first tracked-changes test (the body holds the later author's change).
3a. **Moves** are a deletion and an insertion in two places: two separate suggestions (accepting one alone duplicates or loses the text; the preview says so). Formatting changes are ignored and counted.
4. **Authors are labels.** `suggestions.author_label` (migration 1820), `author_id` NULL, kind `label` in the API; a comment keeps the name in `comments.guest_name` with `share_id` NULL (no link or account makes that). Nothing is notified. Names are stripped of control and direction-overriding characters and cut at 100.
5. **Times**: a Word date is parsed, bounded to 1995 and later, and clamped to the import's time when it is in the future; anything else is the import's time (`db.Now`).
6. **Caps**: 300 suggestions for a document, 1,000 for an import; the rest are accepted and counted. The project's own limit of 2,000 open suggestions refuses an import that would pass it, whole, with a message (an import is one transaction): nothing a reviewer proposed is dropped to make room.
7. **Comments**: Word comments become comments (up to 2,000). A comment is placed on its range when that text is found exactly once, else on the document. Comments in headings, tables, footnotes, comments nothing refers to, empty comments and comments the review domain would refuse to hold are not imported (counted); replies' threading is not kept (each is its own thread).
8. **The token covers them**: a hash of the mode, the suggestions and the comments is mixed into the preview token, so a preview of one mode cannot be applied as another, and the preview's `suggestions` and `comments` are what apply writes (a test asserts it, including with a chapter left out).

## Fix round 3: the PDF is read by a child process

Round 2 hardened one limit at a time and a second review found five more ways past them (1 MB of `trailer(` that did not end in 60 s in the trailer scan, a 31 KB character map that held 2 GB, a 22 MB file that cost 1.1 GB to parse, a piece flood at 1.1 GB and 7 s, a 24.6 MB file that died of a stack overflow). Each is fixed at its cause (below), and, because the next hole should not end the server, the reading of a PDF now happens in a **separate process** with its own memory and time limits. The limits stay as the first defence; the process is the second.

**The process** (`imports/pdfchild*.go`; operator view in [OPERATIONS](../OPERATIONS.md)): the server binary run again as `scriobh pdf-extract` (`imports.MaybeRunPDFChild` is the first call in `main` and in the `TestMain` of every test binary that reads a PDF; a test binary that did not call it refuses to start a child rather than run its tests again). The file goes in on stdin, one JSON document comes out on stdout (64 MiB at most), stderr is discarded, the environment is the marker, `GOMEMLIMIT` (three quarters of the ceiling), `GOGC=50` and the wall clock. The child gets its own process group; the supervisor kills the group with SIGKILL at 20 s, at 512 MiB resident (polled every 75 ms), at 64 MiB of output, when the request is cancelled, and at server shutdown (`StopPDFChildren`, called from `server.Run` before the streams stop). `RLIMIT_CPU` everywhere on Unix, `RLIMIT_DATA` on Linux only (compiled and vetted for linux, freebsd and windows; **the Linux limit, Pdeathsig and /proc polling were not run on this macOS machine**). macOS and the BSDs honour neither RLIMIT_AS nor RLIMIT_DATA for a Go program, so there the polling (75 ms granularity, so a child can overshoot between polls) and GOMEMLIMIT are the limit; on Windows only GOMEMLIMIT. The supervisor reads all of the child's output before it calls `Wait` (which closes the pipe: a read after it lost the end of an answer once, under load, as "its reader gave no answer"). The child does the whole read (`Open`, `ReadPages`, `Analyse`); the parent turns the report into Markdown. A crash, a stack overflow, a kill, a hang or a flood of output is "the PDF could not be read". One person has one PDF being read at a time (a second is a 429); the read is inside the import slot the op already holds, so there are never more children than `SCRIOBH_IMPORT_CONCURRENCY`. Two settings lower the ceilings (`SCRIOBH_PDF_TIMEOUT_SECONDS`, `SCRIOBH_PDF_MEMORY_MB`; they can only lower). Test tests: five misbehaving children (crash, allocation, hang, output flood, one that ignores SIGTERM and leaves a grandchild) leave no process and no goroutine behind; a child dies with its request and with the server; one person cannot have two.

**In the server** (`server/rest.go`): bodies of 8 MiB and over (a PDF in a JSON body is 43 MB as base64) are counted together, 192 MiB for the process, each charged twice its Content-Length (the text and what it decodes to); one that would pass the budget is answered 429 before it is read. The body is read into a buffer of its declared size and dropped after it is decoded when no Idempotency-Key needs it.

**The causes**, with the one change for each:

- *Parse budget*: the lexer charges an estimate of memory (scalars 48 bytes, an array 96 and 16 an element, a dictionary 320 and 100 a key) against 32 MiB while it parses, so an object as large as the file stops where it is. Arrays of more than 1,000,000 elements are refused. Tree walks (page tree, name tree, outline, destinations) spend a shared work budget of 2,000,000 element visits and 50,000 nodes.
- *Character maps*: an entry is charged by its bytes against 24 MiB for the file (destinations longer than 64 bytes are dropped, 4,096 trailers at most), the clock is asked during a parse, and codes are found by binary search.
- *Rebuild by scanning*: one pass over the data in windows, cancellable; a dictionary is read to where its stream starts, `/XRef`, `/ObjStm` and `/Encrypt` are found in the raw bytes, a name written with `#xx` is parsed (at most 50,000 of them; more is "damaged"). Only the last cross-reference dictionary that names a root is parsed. An encrypted file hidden behind 300 decoys or an escaped name is still refused.
- *Filter chains*: `Object` stops at depth 12 (the object-stream to filter to object chain no longer recurses once per link; the 420,000-link file ends in 0.4 s with a refusal and the child's stack never grew).
- *Pieces*: 150,000 for the file (was 600,000), counted as they are read; later pages are not read and the page says so. Pages that were not read (cut off) are not counted as scanned pages in the decision "no text layer".
- *Word*: see "Changes that are close" above.

**Measured** (darwin/arm64 laptop, 48 GB, Go 1.27.4; each case alone in its own process through `import.preview`, the real route: `TestMeasureHostilePDFsThroughTheChild` with `PDF_MEASURE=1`. The child's peak is the kernel's figure (`ru_maxrss`) for the ended process; the parent's heap is `HeapAlloc` sampled every 5 ms, and the parent's peak RSS is `ru_maxrss` of the test process, which includes the test's own copy of the file. The machine was shared with other jobs (load average about 13 during the piece-flood runs, so times are upper figures). Everything in the table was observed in these runs):

| File | Child ran | Child peak RSS | Parent peak RSS / heap | Answer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

The piece flood is the slow one. In process and unloaded it takes 5.1 s (`ReadPages` 1.4 s, `Analyse` 3.7 s); with the child's `GOGC=50` and `GOMEMLIMIT` 6.5 s; in the child under load 7.4 to 8.2 s. A profile shows 35% in the garbage collector and 17% in `assembler.appendLine`, which grows a paragraph of up to 160 KiB by string concatenation a line at a time (quadratic in the paragraph's length). **That was not fixed**: the memory is bounded (150,000 pieces at about 1.9 KB each is about 290 MB, as measured) and the time is inside the 20 s wall, but one such 2.4 MB file holds its owner's one place and an import slot for 5 to 8 s. The memory the server holds for PDFs in the worst case is two children at the 512 MiB ceiling (1 GiB, outside the server's heap) plus the bodies in flight (budget 192 MiB, each charged twice, and measured at 640 MB of sampled heap for two 41.5 MiB bodies, so the budget is an estimate of the bodies' cost, not a hard bound on the heap).

**Not measured**: the Linux limits (`RLIMIT_DATA`, Pdeathsig, `/proc` polling: compiled and vetted for linux, freebsd and windows, not run); a child that overshoots 512 MiB between two polls on macOS (the 75 ms poll bounds time, not bytes; `GOMEMLIMIT` at 384 MiB makes the Go heap work to stay below it, but it is a soft limit). Under `-race` the child (instrumented) used 858 MB and 17 s for the 31 MiB file, so that one test raises the child's ceiling and wall clock for itself.

## Fix round 2: ceilings and what changed (numbers superseded by round 3 above; the ceilings quoted here were lowered)

An independent review found that the limits were counts, each fine alone, while their products were not (a 3 KB file that never ended, a 2 MB file at 22 GB). Changes, and the five reproductions at the review's sizes, re-measured with a sampler of the heap in use (not only what was allocated):

| Reproduction | Before | Now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a 3 KB name tree whose node lists itself 501 times (and the outline's destination tree) | not finished after 20 s, holds an import slot | 0.13 ms (a node is visited once; the file shares 50,000 nodes and the deadline; the Dests tree is read once into a table) |
| 1.9 MB file, 800 object-stream pairs at one 1,000,000-element array | 22.2 GB peak, 20 s | 50 MB, 39 ms (each offset is parsed once; 16 MiB of object text may be parsed for the whole file; an array holds at most 1,000,000 elements); 60 object streams of distinct 250,000-element arrays: 230 MB, 0.24 s |
| 400 fonts naming a ToUnicode map of 100,000 entries each | 2,347 MB | 35 MB, 36 ms (a map is read once for each stream object; all maps share 600,000 entries; /W and /Widths share 400,000 cells; a font is read once for each object; direct font dictionaries count against the 3,000) |
| 1300 pages x 9,400 one-letter pieces (202 KB) | 5,867 MB, 11 s | 117 MB, 0.36 s (pieces are counted as they are read: 30,000 for a page and 600,000 for the file; the text after that is not decoded and later pages are not read; the page says so) |
| 20,000 draws of a 1 MB form (1.1 MB file), 1 s limit | 109 s, 242 GB allocated | 1.0 s, 2 MB (the clock is asked every 2,048 tokens and after each form; what is interpreted is paid for by the byte, 256 MiB for the file; stored stream data is charged to the inflate budget on every read) |

Ceilings that follow: object text parsed 16 MiB (about 250 MB at the worst amplification of a boxed number), character maps 600,000 entries (about 60 MB), pieces 600,000 (about 300 MB through the layout, which now drops the pieces of a page once its lines are built), content interpreted 256 MiB, and the 90 s deadline over all of it. A `/Length` held in another object is read through three levels at most (a chain of 200,000 streams no longer recurses; the stack stays under 64 MB, and the test runs under `-race`). Every number that becomes an int goes through one clamp (`fint`), a length is compared with what is left of the file before it is added to a position, and a glyph whose coordinates are not finite is not placed. An encrypted file is refused when its cross-reference stream or trailer is found by the rebuild (the dictionary is read to where its stream starts, not 400 bytes, and every trailer and every `/Type /XRef` dictionary is checked for `/Encrypt`). Line and paragraph separators and the bidirectional embedding, override and isolate controls are removed from every string taken from a PDF or a Word file (titles included); the directional marks and the letters of a right-to-left script stay. `cmap` and width lookups are searches, not scans (500,000 glyphs through 100,000 ranges: 72 ms). A PDF of 31 MiB through a JSON body: preview 2.3 s, peak heap 502 MB.

A Word author is shown with its origin everywhere a review list draws it: "Ann (from a Word file)" (`rq.word_name` in the page `internal/server/reviewui.go` draws, `review.js.word_name` in `review.js` and `suggest.js`, one change to `who()` that both use); a label cannot begin "Guest:" (it is trimmed) and is "Word" when empty. A test checks that no label renders as a bare name, including "Owner" and "Guest: Bob".

## Limits

PDF (`pdfread.DefaultLimits`; each has a test in `hostile_test.go` and the op-level ones in `pdf_ops_test.go`): file 32 MiB (op body limit), 1,000,000 objects, 3,000 pages, 20,000 page-tree nodes, nesting 48, a stream 32 MiB inflated, 384 MiB inflated (and stored stream data read) for the file, 32 MiB of estimated parsed memory (while parsing; round 3), 2,000,000 element visits and 50,000 nodes in name trees, destination trees and the outline, an inflation ratio of 30 beyond 512 KiB, 600,000 operators for a page, 30,000,000 for the file, form depth 6, 3,000 fonts, 100,000 entries in a font's character map and 24 MiB of map text in all, 400,000 glyph widths, 150,000 pieces of text for the file, 256 MiB of content interpreted, 1,000,000 objects accepted in a rebuild, 20,000 outline items, 48 MiB of text, 20 seconds for the whole read (round 3: the child's wall clock, which is also the file's own deadline; `pdfread`'s own default of 90 seconds is only what the reader's tests and in-process use get; the deadline is a deadline on the caller's context, the file's own deadline is checked while objects are loaded, streams are decoded, content is interpreted and the file is scanned), 30,000 pieces of text for a page (more are left out and the page says so), a search for the end of a stream of unknown length within eight times the file's size, a paragraph cut after 24 KiB at a sentence end and 160 KiB anywhere (so text with no indent and no space does not become one block of the book). The whole read runs in a child process (round 3: 20 s, 512 MiB, 64 MiB of output) and is also bounded by the importer's concurrency limiter (`limiter.go`, two at once) and one PDF at a time for a person and the intake of a preview is kept for the apply that follows (the cache key now covers the PDF's bytes).

Word: the existing limits of the DOCX reader plus 300 and 1,000 suggestions, 2,000 comments, 600-byte quotes.

### What the PDF importer recovers and does not (from the measurements above)

| Recovered | How well |
| --- | --- |
| Words | exact in the round trip; real files: ligatures and unusual characters in CFF fonts with no `ToUnicode` are unmapped and counted |
| Paragraph breaks | exact with first-line indent or space between; in ragged text with neither, a break is missed |
| Chapters and parts | exact from an outline; from headings, exact in the tests, a guess on real books |
| Scene breaks | exact for ornaments and `* * *`; space-only breaks are a heuristic and counted |
| Bold and italic | kept from the font's name and flags; a font that says neither shows none |
| Footnotes | linked when the raised marker is found; unlinked ones follow the paragraph they sit under |
| Lists, quotes, code, inline code | recovered in a print PDF from bullets and numbers, indentation and monospace faces; a PDF that sets them differently gives paragraphs |
| Running heads, page numbers, contents | removed by the stated rule; a head on fewer than 3 pages stays; an index set with dot leaders is removed as a contents page (the report lists the pages: `bashref.pdf` lost its table of contents on pages 3 to 6 and its index on pages 149 to 158) |
| **Not recovered** | |
| Tables | rows only, cells joined by ` | `; no header row, no merged cells |
| Pictures | none imported (counted) |
| Links, underline, colour, size | lost (link text stays) |
| Scanned pages | refused (most pages) or listed as picture-only pages |
| Three or more columns, right-to-left order, vertical and sideways text | not read as such: the first is read as one column and flagged when ambiguous; the last two are left out and counted |
| A footnote that continues on the next page | its second part is appended to the note, not checked |
| Encrypted files | refused |

## Not verified

- **No real Word, LibreOffice, Chrome print or InDesign PDF was available.** The five producer files are imitations assembled by hand with the structure those programs write (font kinds, encodings, `TJ` kerning arrays, flipped matrices, marked content); a real file may differ. Real files that were read: dvipdfm, Ghostscript and Quartz (above).
- No real Word file with tracked changes was available; the Word files in the tests are hand-written XML. The accept-all invariant is tested through the real review ops, but the screens that will show label authors and the acceptance of an imported suggestion in a browser were not built or run.
- The cost of 1,000 suggestions across documents was not timed; the worst single case, 275 in one megabyte chapter, was (above).
- CFF (`FontFile3`) built-in encodings are not read; drop caps, small capitals and text laid out by hand with many separate `Td` moves were not tested with real files.

## What a screen needs

- **Preview** (`import.preview` with `pdf` and optionally `docx`+`docx_changes`): `layout` `pdf`; `report.pdf.report` (the fidelity report: `confidence`, `page_detail` list of `{page, confidence, notes}`, counts, `running_head_rule`), `report.pdf` (producer, damaged streams, ignored actions); `warnings` (sentences, already in English); `nodes` to edit by key as for any import. For a Word file: `suggestions`, `comments`, `report.docx.tracked` (counts of each kind taken as accepted, authors with counts) and a radio for `docx_changes` (the screens' words are `imp.*` and `imp.js.*`; the `import.tracked.*` keys this note once named were not used and are gone).
- **Refusals** come as `invalid` errors with the plain message (scanned, encrypted, not a PDF, damaged).
- **Apply**: `ApplyOut.suggestions`, `.comments`, `.warnings`. A conflict (409) when the project's open-suggestion limit would be passed. The Review screens show a Word author with kind `label` (a name, not a link to a person).
- The CLI's `scriobh import` prints the same warnings.
