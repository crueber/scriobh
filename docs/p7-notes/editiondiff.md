# Phase 7: edition diff (section AU)

Branch `p7-diff`, built on `p7-epub` and since merged with `phase-7` (the EPUB branch as reviewed and fixed, the passphrase gate, and the rest of Phase 7). Backend and ops only: ops, REST, the command line, tests, documentation. Rows: [FEATURES § AU](../FEATURES.md#au-implementation-status-phase-7-edition-diff). It builds on the provenance store of [the EPUB notes](epub.md). Nothing in the EPUB branch's later rounds changes what the loaders assume: a chapter over a document's 2 MiB is not in an edition (so a baseline is never larger than the diff's own chapter cap), a book is read as at most 5,000 chapters (the diff's chapter cap), and `imports.ReadEpub`'s `too_large`, its own ceiling and its use of the caller's context as the deadline arrive as the errors and the cancellation the ops already pass on.

## What it does

Four ops, group `edition` (package `internal/domain/editiondiff`, registered after `docs` and `review` because it writes through their seams):

| Op | Route | Who | What |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `edition.diff` | `GET /api/v1/projects/{id}/editions/{edition_id}/diff?chapter=` | readers (the steps); editors, and a write-scope key (`chapter`, the text) | The project as it stands against one of its editions, chapter by chapter (F-373). |
| `edition.compare` | `GET .../compare?with_project=&with_edition=&source=&sections=&pages=&chapter=` | editors of both projects, and a write-scope key | One edition against another, of one project or of two the caller can open, without importing either (F-377). |
| `edition.suggest` | `POST .../editions/{edition_id}/suggest` | editors | Differences of one chapter turned into suggestions, one each. |
| `edition.take` | `POST .../editions/{edition_id}/take` | editors | A whole chapter put back as the edition had it, as a revision. |

`edition.diff` and `edition.compare` return the same `Result`: `mode`, `base` and `head` (what each side is: edition or project, label, hash, chapters, words, and `source`: `file`, `record` or `project`), `steps`, `summary`, `too_different`, `partial`, `notices`. A **step** is one stop of the review and has `index`, `key`, `status` (unchanged, edited, moved, split, merged, added, removed), `base` and `head` (lists of chapter refs: none for an added or removed step, several for a merged or a split one), `match` (`method`, `score`, `similarity`, `guess`), the flags `renamed` and `moved`, `words` (`base`, `head`, `added`, `removed`), `compared`, `note`, `changes`, `offer` (project mode: what can be done with it) and, for the step named by `chapter` only, `hunks`.

The first text is always the older one: the edition in the path. The second is the project as it stands (`diff`) or the second edition (`compare`).

## Decisions

**How a reviewer steps through the differences.** Taken as recommended, with the reasons. A diff is *read-only* and is cut into units a person can decide on one at a time: the steps (chapters), and inside a step the **hunks** (runs of paragraphs the two texts do not share). Deciding is then done with machinery that already exists and already has its refusals:

- *Offer as a suggestion* (`edition.suggest`): a hunk becomes a pending suggestion on the current document (`review.Propose`, a one-line seam over `suggestCreate`: the same caps, the same checks of the text, the same refusals as `suggestion.create`), reviewed in the review queue, accepted or rejected one at a time, undoable through the revision it makes. One suggestion for each hunk, because a suggestion applies whole or not at all: a suggestion of ten hunks is one decision. At most 20 hunks a call (`more` in the answer counts the offerable ones left, so a caller knows to call again), because each suggestion tells every owner and editor of the project (the existing notification; it cannot be suppressed from here without changing the review domain), and an open-suggestion cap exists (2,000 a project). **A repeat is skipped, not made again**: a difference whose replacement is already an open suggestion on the document (same place, same text replaced, same new text) is reported `Already suggested`, so a retry or a loop makes nothing new. **One notification for a call**, to the owner and editors, naming the first suggestion made (`review.ProposeQuiet` and `review.NotifyProposed`; `suggestion.create` still tells each time). Each suggestion commits on its own, so the call is not atomic: when a later one fails for a reason that is not about the hunk (the cap, a save that came in), what was made is returned in `created`, `stopped` says why, and the remaining hunks are in `skipped`; a failure before anything was made is the error itself, so a retry cannot duplicate a suggestion. The caller is an editor, not the reviewer role `suggestion.create` needs, since the person is making the text of an edition into suggestions on a project they edit.
- *Keep mine / take theirs* (`edition.take`): "keep mine" is not calling it; "take theirs" replaces one chapter's text with the edition's through `docs.ReplaceBody`, the write `revision.restore` makes (the text replaced is kept first as a snapshot, the new version has reason `restore`, the alternatives, ghosts, links, mentions, attachments and review comments follow, and **no goal credit**: the words were written before; `doc.update` would have credited them). `dry_run` previews it. Both writes check `base_revision`.
- *Nothing is applied without an explicit action*: the diff and the compare write nothing at all.

What a suggestion can say is limited by the review rules, and the answer reports it per hunk instead of failing the call: the review domain keeps a document's structure, so a suggestion can **reword inside a paragraph or add paragraphs, and cannot take a whole paragraph out**. So a hunk the edition has and the project lacks (the writer cut it) is offered as an inserted paragraph after the paragraph before it, and a passage only the project has (the writer added it) is **not offered** (`offer_refused` says why; the writer deletes it, or takes the chapter). A hunk whose edition text carries a link, a picture, a heading, a marker or a character the document would not accept is skipped with the review's own message. A chapter the import made into scenes, a split chapter, a merged one and an added or removed one are not offered by hunk. Each hunk says in `offer_refused` whether it would be accepted by the text checks, when the chapter is asked for; the structure checks run when the suggestion is made, so a hunk can still be skipped then, and is.

Taken whole, a chapter that is paired one to one, or merged into one document (the base chapters are joined as the import joined them), can be taken; a split, an added or a removed chapter cannot (a removed chapter is brought back by importing the edition's chapter, `import.append`). A chapter with a picture the project does not hold is refused.

**Reuse of the diff engine.** The paragraph pass and the word pass are `revisions.Diff`'s: `edition.diff` calls it on the chapter's text at paragraph unit, then again at word unit on each reworded hunk, so a hunk's words are what `revision.diff` would say. No second diff algorithm was written. Two additions to `internal/domain/revisions/diff.go`, both small: `Budget` and `DiffWithin`. `revisions.Diff` bounds one call (1,000 edits, 30M comparisons), which is not enough for a book of 5,000 chapters: each call may use all of it, so the cost grows with the number of chapters. `DiffWithin` takes the work left in a shared allowance as its limit and subtracts what it used, and an exhausted allowance degrades a diff to coarse exactly as the per-call limit does. `Diff` itself is unchanged in behaviour (its tests pass). What *is* new is the matching, which is not a diff: pairing chapters with a diff of every pair would be quadratic, so the matcher fingerprints each chapter by the hashes of its runs of three words and finds partners through an index.

**What is compared.** The first text is the baseline of each chapter (the Markdown the converter made, before the writer's edits). The second, for the project, is each live manuscript document's body (`comanchor.Body`: LF, before its alternatives comment), so the offsets of a hunk are the ones `suggestion.create` expects. To make a project that was left alone compare as unchanged:
- pictures: a baseline names a picture `scriobh-picture:<sha256>`, a document `/files/att_...`; the baseline is rewritten to the project's own attachment with that hash (a picture the project lacks stays as the hash, and shows as a difference that cannot be offered);
- chapters the import merged (`merge_into`): the base chapters that name one document are joined with `imports.MergeBodies`, the import's own function (made exported for this: one line in `plan.go`), footnote renumbering included;
- chapters the import split into scenes (`split`): a mapped document's scene children that came from the same import (`document_sources.source_id`) are folded back into its text as `## title` and the scene's text, the way `splitInto` took them apart;
- texts are compared without the blank lines at their end, and every line ends the same way; a difference of spacing only is not a difference.
`TestAnImportedBookLeftAloneIsUnchanged` (a picture, a footnote, a merged pair, a chapter of scenes) is the test that fails if any of these drifts.

**Editions appended into one project.** A project can hold several editions (`import.append`), each with documents of its own. Three rules keep one from being mistaken for another: a document's source path counts only when the import that made it is the one that made the edition (`document_sources.source_id` is the edition's `source_id`: two books from one tool name their files alike, and a shared name is not provenance); `too_different` asks both sides to be mostly unpaired, not either; and a leftover chapter that is a copy of text its neighbour in the pairing already holds is another chapter, not a part of it (a split needs the parts to hold what the first part does not). So a second edition's documents come out as **added** in a diff against the first (the project has chapters the edition does not), and the two editions are told apart by `edition.compare`, inside one project or across two (`TestEditionsAppendedIntoOneProject`).

**Matching, in order.** (1) *recorded*: `edition_chapters.document_id` names a live document of the project (score 1; several chapters naming one document is a merge). (2) *path*: the document's `document_sources.source_path` is the chapter's, from the same import (score 0.95; between two editions the same file name is a guess and is accepted only when the text or a title that is not a position agrees: score 0.6 to 0.95). (3) *title*: a title that names one chapter on each side, and is not a position, pairs them even if the text was rewritten throughout (score 0.6 to 0.95 by the text they share); a title used more than once needs 10% of the text in common; a position ("Chapter 3", "Part II", "Untitled") needs 30%; two empty pages with one name pair. (4) *similarity*: the rest, best first, when they share 35% of their runs of three words (score is that share). (5) *splits and merges*: a leftover chapter of the second text that is at least 60% the text of one chapter of the first is a part of it (the first part may still be paired with it); two or more leftover chapters of the first that are 60% in one of the second are merged into it. A refrain (a run of words in more than 8 chapters of either side, such as a repeated line) is left out of the sizes and of the overlap, so a refrain-heavy book does not hide which chapter is which. Each step says how it was paired (`method`, `score`, `similarity`), and `guess` is true for everything the import did not record. A step with several chapters (merged, split) is as sure as its **weakest member**: the weakest method, the lowest score, and a guess if any member is one (a chapter folded into a merged step by similarity makes the step a guess, however sure the first chapter was). Taking a guessed chapter needs `confirm_guess` (`offer.confirm`; 409 `guess_unconfirmed` otherwise). A rename is a change of the title as written, whatever its case or punctuation (`renamed`, status `edited`); the normalised title is for matching only: **a pairing by title or similarity is a guess, and a screen must show it as one**. The score is a confidence made from the method and the text shared; it is comparable between guesses and is not a probability.

**Statuses.** `unchanged`: paired, same text and title, in its place. `edited`: the text or the title changed (a rename alone is `edited` with `renamed` and no words: the summary does not hide it). `moved`: the text is the same and it is somewhere else (outside the longest run of paired chapters that keep their order, or under another parent); an edited chapter that also moved is `edited` with `moved: true`, so neither fact is lost. `split` and `merged`: one chapter and several. `added` and `removed`: no partner. Words are counted as `revision.diff` counts them, **words added and words removed, never a net figure**: a cut is not a loss, a rewrite is not a gain, and the summary has no net.

**Compare, from the files.** `edition.compare` reads each edition's source file again with `imports.ReadEpub`, the same sections and pages for both, so the two books are cut the same way and a chapter is the same unit in each. When either file is not kept (`has_file` false, or the file is missing or damaged) `source=auto` (the default) compares the text each import recorded, for both, and says so in `notices` and in each side's `source`; `source=file` refuses (409) and `source=record` skips the files. A file that cannot be read (not a book, a zip bomb, DRM) is refused (422) with the import's own reason, and is *not* silently replaced by the records. Records are the right base for `edition.diff` (the baseline is stored so improving the converter does not change what an old import is compared to; see the EPUB notes) and the files are the right base for `compare` (two books from the same tool cut by the same reader).

**Authorization (second round: text needs an editor and, for a key, the write scope).** The registry authorizes and gates the path project (reader for the reads, editor for the writes; a project in the trash is read-only: `edition.suggest` and `edition.take` answer 409 `project_trashed`). Any response that holds the text of an edition (`hunks[].base` and its segments, anything of a baseline) needs the **editor role (or owner) on every project involved and, for an API key, the write scope**, the rule of `edition.download`: the edition is the book as it was received, which includes what the project never held (a chapter left out at import) and what its history may have lost. So:
- `edition.diff` without `chapter` (statuses, counts, titles of the chapters that are in the project) is for readers; with `chapter` it is editors with write scope (403 otherwise, not a filtered answer). The first round let readers step in on the argument that they read the documents and their history; that was wrong for a chapter left out at import and for text the history no longer has.
- **A chapter that became no document at import is not shown below the editors**: not its text, not its title, not its path. For a caller below the rule it is dropped from the edition before the pairing starts, so it cannot pair with a new document of the same title and cannot change what a reader sees; the edition then shows fewer chapters (`base.chapters`). **What stays inferable:** that something was left out is not hidden completely. The `ord` and `parent_ord` of the chapters that remain keep their gaps (they are the book's own numbers; a chapter's `ord` is also in the step key `b<ord>:...`), the edition's recorded size, hash and metadata (`edition.get`) describe the whole book, and the project's own documents show where one is missing. Renumbering was not done: the numbers are the book's, and a screen that needs a count of what is shown has `base.chapters`. The same holds for `edition.get` and `edition.list` (a small change in `domain/editions/ops.go`: below the rule they list only the chapters that are documents and count only those).
- `edition.compare`, with or without `chapter`, is editors of both projects and write scope, declared in its registration (`RequireProject(editor)` and `Scope: write`, as `edition.download`'s), so the role and scope sweeps and the registry refuse it before the handler looks at its input; the second project is checked in the handler (it reads two books and computes; a reader could otherwise trigger it by GET). The second project is asked by hand: membership (404 for a stranger, before the gate), then the passphrase gate once (403 `project_locked`, or keys refused), then the role. A person runs **one comparison at a time** (429 `rate_limited`, "another comparison is still running", `retry_after` 5) and each book takes a place in the import limiter's slots while it is read (`ReadEpub`), so comparisons cannot hold the slots of importers for longer than one conversion; a comparison from the records takes no slot, and the one-at-a-time rule is what bounds it. The limit is in memory, for the process. The other diff ops have no per-person concurrency limit: each loads one project (up to 96 MiB a side); the server's request limits are the only bound, and it was not measured under concurrent load.
- `edition.suggest` is editors (write scope as a POST). Its answer carries ids, counts and refusal reasons and no text (the reasons are the review domain's fixed messages). The suggestion it makes carries edition text and reviewers can read suggestions, so it is an editor's act, and **it is not offered for a chapter that was left out at import** (those can only be taken, by an editor). Rate cap: 10 calls per person per project per 10 minutes (429 `rate_limited` with `retry_after`; a dry run is not counted; in memory, for the process).
- **MCP:** the `edition` group is narrowed op by op (`onlyOps`): `edition.list`, `edition.get` and **`edition.suggest`** are offered (it needs editor and write scope, writes only suggestions that wait for a person, and its answer has no text); `edition.diff` (returns edition text with `chapter`), `edition.compare` and `edition.take` are excluded by name with their reasons. An agent that wants the differences reads the project's documents and their history, which it is already allowed to.
- **The passphrase gate:** `TestGateOpSweep` walks all four ops; `edition.compare` asks the second project's gate itself (`TestAComparisonAsksTheGateOfBothProjects`: locked 403, stranger 404, a key refused).

## Limits

| What | Limit |
| --- | --- |
| Chapters a side | 5,000 (an edition records at most that); more is refused (422) |
| Text a side | 96 MiB in all (refused before any matching, while it is being read: a project is checked by the sum of its documents' sizes, an edition as its baselines are read) |
| One chapter | 2 MiB (a document's own cap): a larger chapter is compared by equality only (`compared: not_compared`, `note`) |
| Lines of one chapter that are diffed | 250,000 a side; more is `not_compared` |
| Work of all the diffs of a comparison | 60,000,000 comparisons, the linear cost of each chapter's lines (8 each) and bytes (1/16) charged to it; spent, the remaining differing steps are `not_compared` and `partial` is true; deterministic (not a clock) |
| Matching index | 600,000 runs of three words for both sides together (beyond it a sample, the same on both sides, and a notice); 8,000,000 index steps; a run in more than 8 chapters is a refrain and is skipped |
| Two texts that share almost nothing | when neither text has a tenth of its words in a paired chapter (a small edition in a large project is covered on its own side) and they hold 2,000 together, `too_different` is true and no chapter is diffed (steps are still listed as paired, added or removed) |
| `edition.suggest` | 20 hunks a call; the review domain's caps (50 changes, 50,000 bytes of new text, 2,000 open suggestions) |
| Reading a source file | the import's own limits (32 MB, 10,000 entries, 96 MiB inflated, entities refused, and so on), a place among the conversions the process runs at once, and the request's context |

## What a screen needs

- Project page, Editions: a "Changes since this edition" link per edition (`edition.diff`), and "Compare with another edition" (a picker over `edition.list` of this project and of the projects `project.list` shows; `edition.compare`).
- The list: one row per step in `index` order: status badge, the base and head titles (`base[]`, `head[]`, several when merged or split), `renamed` and `moved` flags, words added and removed, `changes`; `match.guess` shows "A guess" with `match.score`; `compared` other than `full` and `equal` shows `note`. Above it `summary`, and the `too_different` and `partial` banners; `notices` and `base.source`/`head.source` say where each text came from.
- The step: `edition.diff?chapter=<key>`: the hunks in order, each as the edition's paragraphs beside the project's with `segments` for a reworded one (delete runs struck, insert runs marked, not colour alone), `words`, `before` for orientation, and, when `offer_refused` is empty, "Offer as a suggestion" (`edition.suggest` with `hunks: [id]`; the answer's `created` links to the review queue and `skipped` says why). `offer` on the step says whether "Take the edition's chapter" (`edition.take`) is possible and shows a `dry_run` first. "Keep mine" is closing the step.
- Below the editors (and for a read-scope key) `edition.diff` answers the list without the edition's left-out chapters, `chapter` and every use of `edition.compare` answer 403 (`editions.diff.text_for_editors`): show the list, hide the step and the comparison. `offer.confirm` means a confirmation box before "Take the edition's chapter" (`confirm_guess`); `429 rate_limited` with `retry_after` is shown calmly, not as a failure.
- Both writes need `base_revision`: take it from the head document's revision (`head[0].document_id`, `doc.get`).
- Keys are not stable across edits of either text; a screen asks for the list again after a write.
- `i18n`: the keys `editions.diff.*` in `en.json`.

## Screens (ui-7-import)

- The screens page a chapter's differences (50 to a page) and offer suggestions for the page being read, three calls of at most 20; they read `edition.diff` once for the whole chapter, so a chapter of 350 differences is one answer.
- **A paragraph too long to diff at once** (more than `maxHunkTokens`, a chapter set as one block) is now refined sentence by sentence inside `revisions.DiffWithin` (`diff.go`, `splitSentences`), so three one-word edits in a 30,000-word paragraph are three words added and three removed, as the same text in 60 paragraphs gives (`TestOneHugeParagraphIsRefinedLikeTheSameTextInManyParagraphs`). A paragraph with no sentence end, or past the budget, is still counted whole: the screen says so on the chapter and in the totals.
- **`dry_run` of `edition.take` is not offered by the screen.** The differences above the take section are the preview of what a put-back changes, and the confirmation says the current text is saved first; the op's `dry_run` remains for other callers.

## Not built

- **Only EPUB editions.** `editions.kind` allows docx, pdf and markdown, and nothing makes one yet; `edition.compare` reads other kinds from their records only.
- **A chapter-by-chapter apply of all differences at once**, and "accept all": the review queue's `suggestion.accept_all` does it for suggestions.
- **Many chapters to many chapters** (two merged and re-split at once) is not recognised as a group: the pieces pair one to one where they can and the rest is added and removed.
- **A suggestion to take a whole paragraph out** (the review rules do not allow it), and suggestions across several documents.
- **Scenes of a project compared against a flat edition when the writer, not the import, split a chapter** is recognised as `split` by the text and pairs the first part by the record; the writer's own scene documents are not folded back (a split that the writer made is a split).
- **A benchmark entry** in `scriobh bench` (the brief forbids running it now, and the budgets are asserted in the package's tests).

## Not checked, and why

- **Real books.** Every fixture is generated text, or a book generated and exported by this program's own exporter: no file made by Sigil, Calibre, Pandoc, Vellum or Kindle Previewer was compared. The thresholds (35% for similarity, 60% for a part of a chapter, 30% and 10% for titles) were chosen for generated text with the edits of the tests; real prose has more repeated phrases and a copyedit that touches every sentence shares fewer runs of three words than the generated rewording does. A chapter reworded throughout with a changed title and a changed file name is `added` and `removed`, not `edited`.
- **Large books through the sampling**: past 600,000 runs of three words the matching is by a sample, and a book of tiny distinct chapters may then be matched by name only (`notices` says so). The test of 3,000 chapters shows that case is bounded and accounted for, not that it pairs.
- **Imports of other shapes.** The fold of scenes uses the heading as the import wrote it (`## title`); a heading the importer cleaned (a trailing `#`, an escaped character) can show as a small difference in a chapter that was split.
- **A private note or ghosted text in a document** is part of its text here: it counts as words added. The diff reads the body, not the reading view.
- **Memory** was measured as total allocation, not peak resident size, and only on this machine.
- No browser, e2e, bench or desktop smoke test was run (the brief says not to); there is no screen.
- `make ci` and the CSP and contrast scripts were not run.
