# Phase 7.5 screens: the Writing Studio

Branch `ui-75-studio`, cut from `phase-7.5`. The screens for the studio backend (`docs/p7-notes/studio.md`, FEATURES section BB): the dashboard and its pages, the progress email and nudge switches on Settings, the studio section of a project's Goals page, the sprint line of the writing view, a document's target in the tree, and the finished public card page. FEATURES section BF, F-1100 to F-1119.

The reference (`docs/reference/novlr-writing-studio`) was followed for structure: four period cards, a habit-streak card beside a 24-week calendar with Mon/Wed/Fri rows, a goals panel with "N active, N completed", a words-written chart, a trend. It was not followed where it shames or decorates: no orange down arrow, no "decreased productivity" alert, no gradient bars, no emoji (the chart has a flat tint under its line, which is a flat fill, and the invariants allow those).

## Decisions

**One place for goals, and what the old Goals page is now.** The backend has two sources of goals on purpose: studio goals (`studio.goal.*`) and the daily and total goals of a project's Goals page (`goal.set`, which the op says are "changed on the project's Goals page"). `/studio/goals` is the one place that lists every goal a person has and the one place a new goal is made; a project-page goal appears there with its bar and a line saying where it is changed, and its title links to that page. The project's Goals page is kept as it was (it also holds the project's shared target, which is the owner's and is not a personal goal) and gains a section with the person's own weekly words on that project (`studio.project.totals`) and links to the Studio and to a new studio goal with that project chosen. A separate `/p/{slug}/studio` was not built: the op gives days and weeks of one project and nothing else, which is a table, and a second project page about goals would have been the fork the brief warns against. First thing to reverse if wanted: retire the project page's two personal forms in favour of studio goals (it needs a backend decision, because the legacy daily goal is what `daily_stats.goal_hit` freezes).

**Layout.** Nudges, then the four cards (two by two at 900 px and below), then two columns: streak, goals, patterns, sprint on one side and calendar, chart, trend, projects on the other. Below 900 px the columns dissolve and the order is streak, calendar, goals, chart, sprint, patterns, trend, projects. Goals management, sprint statistics, compare and export, and the card have their own GET pages, so the result of an action is at the top of a short page.

**Charts are HTML and SVG from the server; nothing needs script.** Bars are `<progress>` elements (no `style=` is possible under the policy). Sparklines and the chart are inline SVG with classes; the chart's axis labels are HTML around the SVG so they grow at 200% text; the SVG has `role="img"`, a title and a description, a visible sentence under it says what it shows, and the same numbers are a table one press away. A hover over a column or a day shows its figure through a native `title` (the only hover layer; there is no script to make another). The dataviz skill's rules were followed inside the design system: one hue, magnitude by one sequential ramp, thin marks, recessive grid, text in text tokens, a table view; its categorical palette and validator do not apply (one series, one accent).

**The calendar is a table, and a level is a size as well as a tint.** `role="img"` would have hidden the cells, so it is a real table (seven rows named Monday to Sunday, three of them labelled visibly, months as column-group headings) whose cells each hold a hidden sentence ("3 Nov 2026: 420 words, daily goal met"). Levels 1 to 4 are squares of 40, 60, 80 and 100% of the cell at 66, 78, 90 and 100% of the accent; level 0 is the surface with a hairline. Worked out from the tokens, each step against the panel it sits on, lightest to fullest: Paper 3.29, 4.31, 5.82, 7.49 to 1; Ink 3.80, 4.79, 5.95, 7.05; Sepia 3.29, 4.29, 5.66, 7.18; Slate 3.87, 4.83, 5.93, 6.96; Moss 4.23, 5.32, 6.64, 7.83 (every step is at least 3 to 1, the bound for a graphic). `check-contrast.py` checks the tokens themselves (0 failing pairs); it does not know these mixes, and the browser script measures the fullest step in Slate, Moss and Sepia. A goal day has a ring, today a dashed outline. In forced colours the tint is gone and the sizes remain (checked in a screenshot and by the script). 24 weeks fit a 390 px screen at 10 px squares (the script measures it); at 320 px, and in the all-time view, the table scrolls inside its own labelled, focusable region and the page does not scroll sideways.

**A card says what was added, and sets nothing that is not over against anything (fix round).** The comparison under a card is one sentence ("850 more than the 7 days before (+243%)"); there is no arrow and no colour. The first version also said "120 fewer than yesterday" on today's card and "Nothing was written in the 365 days before" under a card with an empty previous period; the review showed both state a gap exactly when someone returns or opens the page in the morning, so today's card now says "more than yesterday" (or "the same") or nothing, and a card whose previous period is empty says nothing. The 7, 30 and 365-day cards keep their "fewer than the N days before" sentence, a complete period against a complete period, which the roadmap asks for, and the percentage is said only of a gain. The script checks that every comparison has the same computed colour. A person with nothing is shown no comparison at all, no "0 writing days", and a line saying how the page fills.

**Nudges are not recorded by looking.** The backend's contract is "post the ids once shown". A GET that wrote would repeat a side effect on reload and could be triggered by a link, so the page shows what the op returns (at most three at a time) until the person presses Dismiss, which posts `studio.nudge.seen` and redirects. The cost, stated plainly: an undismissed nudge stays for the day, and because only a dismissed one is recorded, a person who dismisses nothing can meet more than three different nudges across a day as the day moves (words to go, halfway, met, a streak). Never more than three are on the page at once, and they appear nowhere but the top of the dashboard. Holding the daily limit exactly needs either a small script that posts what was shown, or a server-side "offered" record made by the read; I would take the second, and it is a backend change.

**The sprint line.** In the writing view a sprint is started, stopped and set aside from one menu (a popover), opened from "Sprint…" in the account menu (More on a phone, where the menu is a bottom sheet) or from the command palette ("Start or stop a sprint…"). While a sprint runs on that project, and only then, one text button sits at the right of the status bar, after the spacer; its place is as wide as the longest thing it can say (the three texts are laid unseen under the live one, `.sp-sz`), so a change of its words moves nothing beside it, at 1024 px too (the review measured a 50 px squeeze of the "Today" item at that width with four digits when the first version let the button's width follow its text), and when the bar is too narrow it is the counts at the left that shorten with an ellipsis, before anything can overlap; with no sprint the status bar is exactly as it was. `sprint.js` is not part of the page: it is listed in the page's extensions only when the server sees a sprint running on that project, and otherwise it is fetched when the menu's form is first submitted (a small general hook in `shell.js`: a form with `data-lazy` is handed to the script it names). It looks three times a minute and writes only when the text changes (once a minute), lays the server's own seconds on the page's clock (so a wrong clock does not move the end), says "Time is up. Finish your sentence" and, when the server has closed the sprint, "Sprint finished: N words" in the toasts, which are a polite live region and take no focus. Start, stop and set aside are fetches (never htmx, as `tree.js` explains for the tree): the page does not navigate and unsaved text stays. Without script the same forms post and redirect. The script counts nothing; words are the save loop's.

**On a phone** there is no status bar, so there is no line while typing: the minutes left are the first line of the Sprint menu when the person opens it, and the end is said in the toasts. I did not put a clock into the phone's header, which already holds the title, the save state and two buttons.

**A first version let the status bar wrap** so that the idle "Sprint" button fitted at 200% text. That would have let the bar change height while a selection's count came and went, which is chrome moving while writing, so it was taken out and the idle button with it: the bar holds the line only while a sprint runs. With a sprint running at 200% text-only zoom the bar's last item (the Source switch, also in the toolbar and the palette) can be clipped, as it already is in a narrow window; that state is not walked by the accessibility script (it runs no sprint).

**The PNG button is in the once-only panel, not on the public page.** `card_test.go` requires the public page to hold no `<script`, and that is worth keeping. The owner sees the link once; that panel has Copy, Open, Download as PNG and Download SVG. The PNG is drawn from the same-origin `/card/<token>/card.svg` through an `<img>` (the policy's `img-src 'self'`), onto a 1200 by 630 canvas, and saved through a blob link. The public page keeps its SVG download.

**Flashes and redirects.** One flash shape (`stFlash`) with `_scope: "studio"` and the page's name, taken with `flashTakeFor`, so the Settings page's older unscoped take cannot swallow it and a goals-page message is not shown on the dashboard (tested). Redirects carry no fragment when the page has something to say, because a browser does not autofocus a page opened at a fragment; the message is focused and scrolled to instead.

**Words.** The ops answer some refusals in English; the screens choose catalogue words by reason or field (`studio.ui.err.*`) and fall back to the op's sentence only for a refusal with no key. Dates are written from `studio.month_short.N`, counts are grouped by the page's translator (`stT`), and the ops' message keys are rendered with their count forms. The backend's messages have no full stop and the screens' sentences do; I left the backend's as they are.

## Fix round after the independent review (Phase 7.5)

The review (`ui-75-studio` at 76aa14a) found six critical items, all of them framing, and twelve smaller ones; every number it could hand-count reconciled. What was changed, and why, in the order of its findings:

- **Compare (1).** The presets of `studio.compare` set finished days against the same number of days before: the days of this week before today against the same days of last week; this month's against last month's, both stopping at the shorter month's last day (31 March is 1 to 28 March against 1 to 28 February); the 30 days before today against the 30 before them. Today is not in it (it is on the Today card). The first version of the fix round included today and still read "−300" on a Monday morning, so the second takes it out: a period with no finished day (a Monday, the 1st) is shown so far in one column, with no difference, and a note that the comparison starts tomorrow (`compared: false`). Each side answers its `days`; a note says "the first N finished days of each period" or, for two dates of unequal length, that no difference is worked out, and the page then shows a dash in the Difference column, never a signed figure. The heads say "so far" and "the same days".
- **Cards (2).** As above. The export's Markdown Today row has no change for a day that is not over, and no percentage of a loss.
- **A lapsed run (3).** The dashboard draws no numeral for a run of 0: it says the past month's writing days in its place, then what starts a run; the longest run only when there was one. The card's SVG leads with the same sentence at 30 px and has no "0 days in a row"; the public page's list leaves out a run, a longest run or a month of 0. The progress email leaves out "met your goal on 0 days" and "days in a row now: 0".
- **A project's goal (4).** `today.words` is the headline goal's own value, so a daily goal that belongs to one project is neither met nor short by words written in another; `today.project_id` lets the screen say which project. The Today card (every project) is unchanged. The quick "Write N words today" is offered only when the project it would open is the goal's.
- **A sessions, minutes or chapters goal (5).** `streak.measure`, `today_value` and `today_target` carry that goal's own figure; two messages (`studio.streak.keep_goal`, `.begin_goal`) name no count. The screen says "Today, in sessions: 0 / 1", and draws no `<progress>` without a maximum.
- **The sprint toast (6).** `sprint.js` keeps the sprint's id and looks for it among the stats' recent rows, telling "stopped" or "finished" by its state; one that is not there is not told. After a start or stop from the menu, focus goes to the text (or the sprint line when there is no text to write in). The script's limit went from 2,560 to 2,816 B gzip for this.
- **The status bar (7)**, **RTL labels (8)** (the chart's SVG is mirrored in a right-to-left language, so the line, its labels and the y axis agree), **the key (10)**, **a day's figure (12)** (`studio.js`, new, 1.3 KB limit: a tap or click, or the arrow keys with the calendar focused, writes the day under the calendar; it also scrolls the calendar to its newest week, which was the all-time calendar at 390 px, item 16).
- **The forecast (9).** Read from the net words, as before: a total goal in words is the manuscript's size, so its finish is projected from the manuscript's growth (a pace of added words would project a finish the manuscript will not reach). The rate is worded plainly ("about N net words a day", also for the pace a deadline asks), the unit is in the answer (`forecast.unit`, `basis.measure` `net`), a projection later than the deadline is not counted in days, and with no growth the line is the neutral "No date can be estimated yet from the last weeks of writing", never a shortfall. (A first pass of the fix round read it from the words added, on a brief that was wrong, and was reversed.) AGENTS.md's rule is that net words never fail a day or shame a cut: a forecast does neither, and says only what the manuscript has done.
- **Lows.** The card's footer is `#6B675E` on `#FAF8F3` (5.31:1, was 3.47:1), goal rows are 30 px apart (the third bar no longer touches the date), a project's name is its own `unicode-bidi: plaintext` run so a Hebrew name ends, with its ellipsis, at its own left (the SVG still holds no bidi control character), negatives on the screens use U+2212 (`stNum`; the CSV and JSON keep the hyphen), `ui-studio.md` no longer says "no area fills". Not changed: the footer date is still ISO (`2026-11-16`); the card has no language of its own to write it in.

Tests: `internal/server/review_ui_studio_test.go` (the review's eight, unchanged), `fix_ui_studio_test.go`, `internal/domain/studio/fix_p75_test.go`, `internal/mail/fix_p75_test.go`; `e2e-studio` gained two steps (the sprint toast, focus and the bar at 1024 px; a day's figure, the all-time calendar, compare and right to left).

After the first fix-round pass an advisor's look found and fixed: focus after a sprint start goes to the text only where a fine pointer is (a touch screen is not given its keyboard; focus goes to the More tab instead), the card's lead sentence is sized to the card (a two-digit count and `xx-rtl` tested), the calendar's current-day marker is an outline in forced colours, `#st-counts` clips with `overflow-x: clip` (a `hidden` overflow cut the text at 200% size, which the accessibility walk caught), and compare's table keeps its words whole and scrolls inside its region at 390 px, with a single day written as that day. `scripts/fakehost.mjs` writes its key's curve by name: macOS's `/usr/bin/openssl` (LibreSSL 3.3.6) wrote the parameters in full, Go refused the certificate ("invalid ECDSA parameters") and the accessibility walk's `seed` step failed with "host could not be reached"; with Homebrew's OpenSSL first in `PATH` it passed, which is why the review's run and the builder's differed.

**Third pass (the coordinator's decisions):** the forecast went back to net words with plain labelling and a neutral line for no growth, and compare takes today out (above). The earlier hand-back's two open questions were these.
