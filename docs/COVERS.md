## What it is

Today every cover Scríobh makes is generated: one flat colour, the title, subtitle, author and kind of book (F-030, F-230, F-593). A **cover layout** is the writer's own cover. When a project has one, it takes the place of the generated cover everywhere a cover appears. When it has none, nothing changes.

A layout is one of two shapes:

1. **Front and back.** Each side is one of:
   - **generated**: today's typographic cover for that side;
   - **image**: one picture, full bleed, filling the side;
   - **page**: a picture placed on a page of the book's trim, with a flat background colour, a fit (fit inside, fill and crop, actual size), a scale, a position on a nine-point grid, and a margin.

   The back is optional. A project with only a front image is the common case (it is all an ebook ever shows).
2. **One image for the whole wrap.** A single picture that spans back, spine and front, as a printer's wraparound file does. The front and back are cut from it.

The generated cover stays as the fallback and the default. A layout never changes the manuscript's text.

## What other tools do, and what we take from it

From the research (sources in "Sources"):

- **Ebook and print covers are different files.** Vellum, Atticus, Reedsy and KDP all keep the ebook cover (front only) apart from the print wrap. KDP tells authors to leave the back, spine and barcode out of the ebook cover. *We take:* one layout serves both, but the ebook only ever receives the front.
- **Only two tools make a wrap.** Pressbooks generates front, spine and back from page count, paper and text; Draft2Digital generates a wrap from the ebook front, or takes one uploaded file for the whole wrap. Writing apps (Vellum, Atticus, Scrivener, Ulysses) make the ebook cover only and leave the wrap to a designer. *We take:* both of D2D's routes: build the wrap from sides (shape 1), or accept a designer's whole-wrap file (shape 2).
- **A back cover inside a book is just a full-page image.** Atticus and Vellum let a back cover in only as a full-page image in the matter. No standard puts a back cover in an EPUB. *We take:* the EPUB's back cover is an option, off by default, placed as the last page.
- **Low resolution is a warning, never a block, and never upscale.** Vellum, KDP Cover Creator and Apple all say so. *We take:* warnings with the effective dpi at the placed size, as the PDF already does for interior images (`image_low_resolution`).
- **Geometry belongs to the printer, not to us.** KDP and IngramSpark disagree on text margins (0.125 in vs 0.25 in), barcode boxes (2 × 1.2 in vs 1.75 × 1 in), and the spine-text floor (79 vs 48 pages). *We take:* named print profiles (KDP paperback, IngramSpark paperback) with the numbers, and the printer's own template stays authoritative.
- **The spine comes from the page count, and a page count changes.** Pressbooks warns that its wrap is tied to the last PDF's page count. *We take:* a wrap records the spine it was made for, and a change of page count marks it stale; nothing is ever stretched to fit.
- **Manuscripts carry no cover art.** Shunn's format and agents' advice point that way; no source requires it, so this is an inference. *We take:* the manuscript DOCX and the submission bundle never include a cover.

## Where a layout lives

A layout belongs to the **project** (the manuscript), not to any one export: the same cover is wanted in the EPUB, the print wrap, the library and the site. It is stored in its own table, not as a document in the manuscript tree, because a cover is not text: it has no words, no revisions, no goals, no word counts, and the `documents.kind` check would need a rebuild of the largest table to admit it.

It is still **in the manuscript where the writer looks**: the manuscript tree gets a fixed "Cover" row above the first document, which opens the cover page. The row shows a small thumbnail when a layout exists and the generated swatch when it does not. It cannot be moved, trashed or excluded from compile: removing the layout is "Use the generated cover" on the cover page.

(The alternative, cover pages as documents of a new kind, is listed under "Open decisions".)

A **side**: `{"mode":"generated"}`, `{"mode":"image","attachment":"att_…"}`, or `{"mode":"page","attachment":"att_…","fit":"contain|cover|actual","scale":100,"position":"center","background":"#RRGGBB","margin_in":0.5}`. `position` is one of the nine grid names; `background` is one of the cover palette (no free colour, as today); `scale` is 10 to 100 percent of the fit; `margin_in` is 0 to 1.5.

A **wrap**: `{"attachment":"att_…","bleed_in":0.125}`, at the layout's `trim`. The spine is not stored: it is measured from the picture (below).

**The aspect.** Every side, and the front cut from a wrap, has the trim's proportions (6×9 is 2:3). On screens and in the ebook the front is cut **at the trim**, without bleed; the bleed exists only on the print page. An `image` side whose picture has another aspect is placed by `fill and crop` from its centre (an ebook-shaped 1.6:1 picture loses a sliver top and bottom on a 1.5:1 panel), and the cover page shows the crop before it is saved; a writer who wants the whole picture uses a `page` side with `fit inside`. A print cover made for another trim than the layout's is refused with a sentence naming both (`cover_trim_mismatch`): the layout is made again for the new trim, never stretched.

**Images are attachments.** A cover image is an ordinary project attachment, so it is counted in the quotas, archived by backup, served by `GET /files/{id}` with the usual headers, and deduplicated. The `attachment_refs.ref_kind` check gains `cover` (the migration rebuilds that small table), and the layout writes a `cover` ref for each image it uses. The Markdown mirror copies every attachment that has any reference (`mirror/extras.go`), so it carries the cover with no further change. The Git push is narrower: a public remote carries only attachments whose id appears in a pushed document's text (`gitmirror/audience.go`), so the push **needs a change** to carry `cover` references; whether a public remote should carry the cover is decided per remote by its audience (on by default for a public remote: the cover is the book's face, and its bytes are cleaned). Deleting an attachment that has references is already refused unless forced (`ops.ConflictWith`, "still link to this attachment"); a forced delete of a cover image returns the layout to the generated cover for that side.

**Images are cleaned when they become a cover**, not only at publish: `imagesafe.Clean` runs on upload through the cover ops, and `set` accepts only an attachment of the same project that the cover upload made (a `cover_clean` flag on the attachment row, set by `project.cover_layout.upload`). An ordinary attachment, which is stored as uploaded, cannot be named in a layout without going through the cover upload, which cleans it into a new attachment, so the stored bytes have no EXIF, GPS, XMP or thumbnails, and every surface that shows the cover (site, public Git remote, share links, exports, the library) carries cleaned bytes. A CMYK JPEG, SVG, or anything over the pixel cap is refused at upload with a sentence saying why (the PDF writer is RGB only). `set` and its dry run also place every `page` side (`PlacePage`, the check the cover form runs) and refuse a placement that could not be drawn with the same code: `cover_picture_extreme` (a picture over 8 times the page, or under a pixel, at the chosen fit and scale; the scale and margin ranges and the picture itself have their own codes), so REST, the CLI and MCP save nothing the derivative would later fail on.

**Derived images** are cached under `files/projects/<id>/covers/<layout hash>-<name>.<ext>` (the path ARCHITECTURE already reserves), made on first use and swept when the layout changes:

- `front`: the front for screens and ebooks, at most 2560 px on the long side (KDP's and Vellum's figure), JPEG at quality 90, sRGB;
- `back`: the same for the back, when there is one;
- `thumb`: 480 px wide, for the library and the tree row.

They are never the source of truth: the attachment and the layout are. They are not in backups (rebuilt on demand).

`projects.cover_path`, which nothing has ever used, is left as it is and documented as unused (dropping it would rebuild `projects`).

## The wrap: measuring and cutting

A wrap picture's size in inches is known from its height: the height is the trim height plus two bleeds, so `dpi = pixel height / (trim height + 2 × bleed)`. Then:

- `wrap width in = pixel width / dpi`;
- `spine in = wrap width − 2 × trim width − 2 × bleed`;
- the front is the outer `trim width + bleed` on the front's side, the back the outer `trim width + bleed` on the other side.

So the front can be cut with no paper thickness and no page count. For a left-to-right book the front is on the right; for a right-to-left book (the project's language direction) it is on the left. The cut is a crop of the decoded picture, encoded once as the `front` derivative.

At upload the measured spine is shown. A spine under zero, or a dpi under 150, is refused (the picture is not a wrap for that trim). When a print cover is made (`export.cover`), the measured spine is compared with the spine computed from the page count and the paper (`compile.cover_spec`). A difference over 1/32 in is the warning `cover_wrap_stale`, with both figures: the picture is placed as it is and never stretched, and the writer gets a new wrap from their designer or the printer's template.

The width alone cannot tell a wrong trim from a different spine: with the height fixed, any error in the chosen trim's width moves into the measured spine. So the trim is not checked at upload beyond the refusals above. A wrong trim shows as a spine that is negative, or wider than any book could need (over 2.5 in), which is refused at upload, or as `cover_wrap_stale` when a print cover is made, where the computed spine disagrees with the measured one. The cover page shows the measured spine in inches and pages (at the chosen paper) so the writer can see at once that a 6×9 template was loaded as 5.5×8.5.

### The print cover with a layout

`export.cover` keeps its inputs (preset, page count, paper thickness, bleed, guides, barcode) and gains `profile` (`kdp`, `ingram`, `generic`, default `generic`), which sets the text-safe margin, the barcode box size and the spine-text floor:

| Profile | Text margin inside trim | Barcode box (lower right of back) | Spine text from | Spine text from the edges |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `kdp` | 0.125 in | 2 × 1.2 in | 79 pages | 0.0625 in |
| `ingram` | 0.25 in | 1.75 × 1 in | 48 pages | 0.0625 in (0.03125 in under a 0.35 in spine) |
| `generic` | 0.25 in | 2 × 1.2 in | 48 pages | 0.0625 in |

With `sides`, the panels are drawn from the layout and the spine as today (flat colour, rotated title and author, now gated by the profile's page floor as well as the width). With `wrap`, the picture is the whole first page, nothing is drawn over it, and the barcode box is drawn only when asked (the designer may have left room, or the printer adds it). A second page, as today, is the front alone; a third, new, page is a **proof**: the wrap at a reduced size with the trim, bleed, spine folds, text margin and barcode box drawn over it, so the writer can see what a printer will cut. The proof page is never sent to a printer; the guides option keeps its meaning for page one.

The PDF stays RGB (the writer has no CMYK path; see "Not in scope"). The cover check reports the effective dpi of each placed picture (`image_low_resolution` under 300), the stale wrap, and a page-mode picture that crosses the text margin.

**Pixel cap.** A 300-dpi wrap is about 11 megapixels at 6×9 in but 18.5 at US Letter and 19 at A4, over the PDF writer's 16-million cap per picture. The cover's picture gets its own cap of 24 million pixels (this was 40; see "Image processing" for why). A JPEG is passed through a PDF without decoding, so the PDF is not the limit; a PNG wrap over 16 million pixels is refused at upload with the advice to send a JPEG.

## The cover page

`/p/{slug}/cover`, reached from the tree's Cover row, the project menu, and the Compile page's cover section.

- **Shape:** three choices: the generated cover; front and back; one image for the whole wrap.
- **Front and back:** for each side, generated, image or page. Image and page take an upload (a file input; drag and drop on the drop zone with script). Page shows fit, scale, the nine-point position, the background swatches and the margin.
- **Wrap:** an upload, the trim (the print presets' sizes), the bleed; then the measured dpi and spine, and a drawing of the picture with the cut lines, the spine folds and the text margin.
- **Preview:** the front at the library tile's size and at a page's size, both themes. With script, page mode updates as the controls change (an SVG placed by the server's own arithmetic; "The cover page as built" says why it is not CSS `object-fit`). Without script, Save shows the server's rendering.
- **Alt text:** required when a picture is used, with a hint ("what the cover shows, in a sentence").
- **Warnings** in words, each with what to do: low resolution at the chosen trim, a wrap that does not fit the trim, a stale spine, a picture that is not RGB.

Works at 390 px, with the keyboard alone, after a reload with the network cut (checked, and it is not: the shell's cache holds only `/static/` and the offline page, for every project page, so a reload with the network cut shows the offline notice and nothing of the cover; see "The cover page as built"), in both themes, and inside the desktop window. Every word goes through the i18n catalogue (`cover.`). The script is lazy, `cover.js` at most 5 KB gzip, inside the budgets in ARCHITECTURE. The CSS goes into the per-page sheet under the 25 KB per-page budget.

## Image processing

Cutting the front from a wrap and making the `front` and `thumb` derivatives needs cropping and scaling. The standard library crops (`SubImage`) and encodes JPEG and PNG, but has no scaler. The plan adds `golang.org/x/image/draw` (the Go team's pure-Go module; CatmullRom for downscaling), pinned in `go.mod`. No CGO, no ImageMagick, no npm. The alternative, a hand-written box filter, is smaller and looks worse; see "Open decisions". (As built: x/image's filter, run a row at a time; see below.)

What a decode takes was measured through the real op path (upload, set, and the front and thumbnail made: `TestMeasureCoverMemory`, peak resident set size of the test process, about 43 MB of it the process itself, Apple silicon, `GOMAXPROCS=2`). The first design held a 40-megapixel progressive JPEG at 1,045 MB and a 24-megapixel one at 695 MB, from the decoder's coefficients and the scaler's temporary; now:

| Picture | Peak |
|---|---|
| a 24 MP baseline JPEG, 4:2:0 | 109 MB |
| a 24 MP baseline JPEG, 4:4:4 | 147 MB |
| the same with an EXIF rotation (turned at upload) | 273 MB (4:2:0), 309 MB (4:4:4) |
| a 16 MP PNG, RGBA | 173 MB |
| a 16 MP PNG, 16 bits a channel (the worst PNG that is allowed) | 283 MB |
| a 20 MP progressive JPEG, 4:4:4 (the worst allowed: 15 bytes a pixel, 300 MB) | 370 MB |
| a 24 MP progressive JPEG, 4:4:4 | refused at upload with the advice to save a baseline JPEG, or under 20 MP |

How it is held there: the crop is made first (nothing outside it is read); a picture more than twice the size it is drawn at is averaged down by a whole number in a box pass that reads the decoded picture a block at a time; the Catmull-Rom pass (`scale.go`) is `golang.org/x/image/draw`'s filter done a row at a time, because x/image's own scaler keeps a temporary of the output's width by the source's height in four float64s a pixel (140 to 330 MB for a front), and a test holds it to x/image's result (within one level of 255, with transparency and for cuts); a progressive JPEG, which Go's decoder keeps whole as coefficients (4 bytes a sample), is refused above a decode budget (`MaxDecodeBytes`, 300 MB: `imagesafe.DecodeBytes` reads the frame header); a rotated JPEG that the upload has to decode to turn is held to the same budget (`imagesafe.CleanBounded`); and the memory all decodes in flight may take together is itself bounded (`decodeMem`, 600 MB, whatever the compile gate's width: a derivative waits for its share and is then told the server is busy). The upload's rotation decode takes its share of the same budget (`imagesafe.CleanGuarded`: the server is busy, 429, when none is to be had; an upload that decodes nothing is not held up). So the most a server holds for covers at once, uploads and derivatives together, is about two of the worst, 750 MB with the process, at any `SCRIOBH_COMPILE_CONCURRENCY`. (What an upload holds besides is its body: at most the file limit, 25 MB by default, for each of four uploads at once.)

Derivative work runs in the existing compile gate (`config.CompileConcurrency`), one at a time per project (one order everywhere: the project's lock, then a slot, then the memory; a caller already inside the gate, a compile or a site build holding its slot, takes no lock, since its writes need none: a derivative is named by its key, so two makers write the same bytes atomically, and each checks once more after the file is in place that the layout is still the one it was made for), and its time is a bench metric (`cover.derive` p95 under 1.5 s for a 6×9 wrap on the reference machine). The front and the thumbnail are made when a layout is saved, after the commit and inside the gate, so the first look does not wait; a busy server only means they are made when asked for.

## Not in scope

- **A text-over-image designer.** No title or author is drawn over an uploaded picture (KDP Cover Creator does; it is a different product). A page-mode side has a picture and a background, nothing else. The generated cover remains the way to get typeset text.
- **CMYK and PDF/X-1a.** The PDF writer is RGB only. KDP's help says to use CMYK; Ingram asks for it. Many writers' designers deliver the print wrap themselves; the RGB proof and the stale-spine check still help them. Recorded as a known limit, with the printer's upload preview as the check.
- **Hardcover case laminate and dust jackets** (different wrap and hinge geometry per printer).
- **AI cover concepts** (F-211) and any image generation.
- **3D mockups and per-store marketing images.**
- **Covers in the manuscript DOCX or the submission bundle.**

## As built in C1 (the back end)

Built and tested: the migration `0810_cover_layouts`, the package `internal/domain/coverlayout`, the five ops, the wrap's measuring and cut, the derivatives, and the consumers named below. Where the code is not what the sections above say:

- **The cache key.** A derivative is `files/projects/<id>/covers/<key>-<name>.jpg`, where the key is the sha256 of the layout's hash, the version of the rules (`coverlayout.Version`) and the book's direction (`ltr` or `rtl`: a wrap's front is on the other side for a right-to-left book, and the language is not part of the layout), not the layout hash alone. A change of rules or of language then makes new pictures, and the sweep (on `set`, on `clear`, and whenever a picture is written) removes every file of another key. A backup leaves the folder out (`internal/backup`). A sweep takes the project's lock and waits for it however long that takes (whoever holds it is bounded); a temporary file is removed only when it is over ten minutes old; a derivative is kept only if the layout is still the one it was made for, checked before it is written and again after (a file put in place after a change's sweep is taken out by its maker); and a forced delete of a picture sweeps, after the commit, everything made from it. If the process stops between the commit and the sweep, what was made from the deleted picture stays until the next time the project's derivatives are made or swept.
- **`project.cover`** also takes `size=thumb` (the 480 px thumbnail), `part=back` (the back, 404 when the layout gives it no picture) and `v` (a cache-busting value that is ignored; the ETag still decides). The API's `cover_layout.get` answers `front_url`, `back_url` and `thumb_url` with these.
- **The project list and `project.get`** carry `cover_layout` (a front picture exists), `cover_thumb` (the thumbnail's address) and `cover_alt`; a locked project shows none of them. The library tile and the "Continue" line draw `cover_thumb` in a plain `<img>` inside the existing 3:4 box.
- **Compile options.** `own_cover` (EPUB, default true): false uses the generated cover and leaves the project's own cover out of the input hash.
- **Revisions.** `clear` keeps the row as a tombstone (an empty hash) so the revision goes on counting: a writer who read revision 1 and finds the cover cleared and made again meets 3, never 1, and is told to read it again. A writer who saw no layout sends 0, or the revision the cleared one left behind.
- **A wrap's `bleed_in`** may be 0 (a wrap made without bleed); omitted it is 0.125. A `wrap` layout has no `front` or `back`.
- **Upload** takes `trim` and `bleed_in` as query parameters so the resolution and the spine are measured at once, and accepts PNG and JPEG (a GIF and a WebP are refused: a printer wants a PNG or a JPEG, and the PDF writer embeds no WebP). The picture is stored through the importer's own cleaning and staging (`attachments.PreparePicture` and `NewImport`), so a duplicate of cleaned bytes that the project already holds is the same attachment and is marked `cover_clean`.
- **Page sides.** The page is the trim scaled so its long side is 2560 px (a 6x9 page is 1707 by 2560); the margin is in inches of that page. `actual` puts the picture on the page at 300 dpi. A page side's background defaults to the project's cover colour. The nine positions are `top-left`, `top`, `top-right`, `left`, `center`, `right`, `bottom-left`, `bottom`, `bottom-right`. A picture that is taller or wider than the box is clipped at the margin. A picture is never enlarged: `contain` and `cover` place it at its own size when the page's box is bigger (the layout says `cover_picture_small`), so a small picture sits smaller on the page.
- **Input hashes.** An export's input hash holds the cover only where the output shows it: the EPUB with its `cover` option on (the front only: the shape, trim, alt text, the front side or the wrap with the side the language puts the front on, and the pictures' own hashes, so a change to the back alone is the same EPUB), and later the print cover and the PDF's `cover_page` option. A manuscript, a Markdown file, an interior PDF, the submission bundle and an EPUB with the cover off have the hash they had. A project with no layout has the hash it had. The HTML export, which has no input hash, records `cover_layout` (the front's hash) in the export's manifest; its pages hold the cover as a data URI and every page keeps the policy `img-src data:` only (a bundle also has the picture as the file `images/cover.jpg`). The static site's input hash holds the picture's hash, size and alt when the cover is shown. The compile's `language` option turns a wrap the way that language reads.
- **The Markdown mirror** writes `cover/<part>-<sha12>.<ext>` and the layout without ids as `cover` in `project.json`; a cover picture that no document links to is not copied to `attachments/` as well. **The Git push** carries the same only to a remote whose `include_cover` is on: a private remote (a backup, the whole copy) has it on, a public remote has it off, including every public remote that existed before (migration `1837`). Turning it on for a public remote, or for one that was made by the publish flow or sits at an address that was ever public, is a change that lets more reach a public repository (F-861): it needs a person at a browser (a key is refused) and the remote's name typed (`confirm_name`), and the audit entry says it was turned on; turning it off is always allowed, and for a public remote starts the remote's local history over, as `only_done` does, because the earlier commits hold the cover. Turning it on marks the remote behind (an on-save remote then pushes it by itself), and sending the same audience again leaves the setting as it is (only a real change of audience takes the new audience's default). `repo.plan` and `repo.publish` take `include_cover` (a private audience always carries it; a public audience with it on needs the repository's name typed in `confirm`, whatever its visibility), the plan lists the cover's files, the Markdown bundle of a release (`md_bundle`) follows the remote's switch, and so does an EPUB release (the compile option `own_cover` is false unless the switch is on, so the generated cover stands in). `git.remote.get` says `exposed` (public, made by the publish flow, or at an address that was ever public): the Git page shows the name field of its cover and audience forms exactly then. The Git page has a control for it (`/ui/projects/{id}/git/cover`, the owner's). A public remote never carries the alt text.
- **EPUB import** has `use_cover` on `import.apply` (a new project only) and `epub.cover.can_use_as_cover` in the preview. The picture is the one the import stores, already cleaned; the cover checks run on it again, and a picture that fails them stays an attachment with a warning.
- **`project.cover_layout.check`** measures the layout at a trim (the layout's, or `preset`), reports each picture's resolution, the wrap's measured spine, the profile's figures, a `cover_trim_mismatch` and, with a page count and a paper thickness, a `cover_wrap_stale`. It does **not** yet check the text margin, the barcode box or the spine text (`not_checked` says so): those arrive with the print cover in C2.
- **Colour.** A picture's own ICC profile (a JPEG's APP2, a PNG's iCCP) goes into its derivatives, whose pixels are scaled in the picture's own space and not converted, and goes across an EXIF turn (the re-encode would drop it). It is carried only if it is at most 1 MB with `acsp` at byte 36 and the colour space `RGB ` at byte 16; any other is left out, never cut down (so a front, a thumbnail and an EPUB never carry a huge one), and the upload says so (`cover_profile_dropped`). The upload also warns (`cover_profile_not_srgb`) when the profile is not sRGB, since a reader that ignores profiles shows the colours differently.
- **Webhooks.** The events `cover_layout.updated` and `cover_layout.cleared` are in the event log, the mirror and the live stream; the webhook catalogue is a closed list of four and has not been extended, so no webhook can subscribe to them yet.
- **For C2.** The PDF writer's per-picture cap (`publish.MaxImagePixels`, 16 million) applies to what it has to decode; a JPEG is passed through up to 200 million pixels (`maxJPEGPixels`), so a cover JPEG within the 24-megapixel cap is not refused, and a PNG cover is at most 16 million pixels by the upload's own rule, so the cap is never hit either. What C2 must change: nothing for the formats a cover may have (PNG and JPEG are both embedded). The panels come from `coverlayout.MeasureWrap` (`FrontBleed`, `BackBleed`, `Spine`, in picture pixels).

## As built in C2 (print)

Built and tested: `internal/publish/pdf_cover_plan.go` (the plan: profiles, panels, placements, resolution, the warnings; no picture is read to make it), `pdf_cover.go` (the drawing, which follows the plan and adds nothing to it), `internal/domain/coverlayout/print.go` (a layout turned into the plan's input, the pictures read, the decode memory reserved), `internal/domain/compile/cover.go` (`export.cover`, `compile.cover_spec`, the PDF's cover page), `check` in `coverlayout/ops.go`. Rendered to PDF, looked at with `pdftoppm` (poppler, installed for it) at 50 to 110 dpi, and read back by `internal/publish/check`, which was taught to read page boxes, matrices, clips, fills, image sizes and ICC colour spaces so the tests can say exactly what a page holds. Pictures of the renders are in `docs/screens/covers-c2/` (synthetic art with a border, a cross and a circle, so a crop, a bleed and a margin can be seen).

**What each mode produces.** **The file for the printer is exactly one page**, `<slug>-cover.pdf`: KDP and IngramSpark take a single full-wrap page, and a file with more would be refused or have its extra pages read. The front alone and the proof are files of their own, chosen with `export.cover`'s `part` (`cover`, the default; `front`, `<slug>-cover-front.pdf`; `proof`, `<slug>-cover-proof.pdf`, only for a cover with a layout), each recorded as its own export with its own manifest (`part`) and input hash (the printer's file keeps the hash it had; the others add the part). What a project with no layout produces **changed in one way only: it is one page, not two.** That page is the old first page, byte for byte in its content stream (pinned in `TestGeneratedCoverIsWhatItWas`, taken at the commit before C2, at eight spines and sizes); `part=front` is the old second page (the same size, trim box and type in the same places; its content stream names its fonts in the order it first uses them, so its bytes differ alone), and `part=all`, which only tests use, still writes the old two-page file with the digest it had. With a layout, the parts are:

| | `cover` (the page a printer takes) | `front` | `proof` |
|---|---|---|---|
| sides | media box = wrap with bleed; TrimBox inset by the bleed, BleedBox the whole page; the cover colour; the back panel, the front panel, the spine type, the barcode box when asked | the front alone at trim plus bleed on every side | the proof |
| wrap | one picture at the whole page and nothing else (`q w 0 0 h 0 0 cm /Im Do Q`: tested to be the only paint on the page); media box = the picture's measured size, TrimBox inset by the wrap's own bleed | the front cut from it, with the wrap's bleed on its outer edges only | the proof |
| generated side in a layout | the typographic panel as today | likewise | the proof |

- **An image side** is one placement: the picture covers the trim plus the bleed on all four sides, centred on the trim, as small a scale as does that (never enlarged past what covers; no stretch). Each page clips it: page 1 to the panel with the bleed on its outer edges only (the spine side stops at the trim), page 2 to the whole page, so the front is the same picture on every page. A transparent PNG is flattened: laid over white and written with no soft mask (a printer wants a flattened file). Its resolution is the picture's pixels over the size it prints at (the trim plus the bleed). **The print crop is a little tighter than the ebook's:** the ebook cuts the trim's proportions from the centre; the print fills trim plus bleed, so about 2 per cent more of the picture is lost at each edge at 6 by 9 (the bleed is cut off). A 300-dpi 6 by 9 picture (1800 by 2700) is 288 dpi there, and the warning says what size would give 300.
- **A page side** is `PlacePage`'s answer, unchanged: its `Box` and `Dst` in the 2,560-pixel page are mapped to the trim page (per axis, rounded edge by edge), the background fills the panel to the bleed (a transparent picture is flattened on it), the picture is clipped to the box inside the margin. With no margin the clip runs on into the bleed over whatever of the picture lies there (`fit` cover, or actual size): where the picture reaches an outer edge with too little past it, `cover_bleed_missing` says the bleed will show the background colour. A picture is never enlarged beyond C1's rule (one picture pixel to one page pixel, 284 dpi at 6 by 9), so a small picture sits smaller on the print too; that is what the cover page's preview shows.
- **The back `none`** is the cover colour with no type, not the generated back: a project with only a front picture should not get a "NOVEL" label on its back. (A back that is `generated` keeps today's drawing, its kind of book.) **The spine** is the cover colour with the title and author when the plan allows it: at least a quarter inch, at least the profile's pages, not right to left, not a wrap. In a layout (or when a `profile` is named) the type is sized from the clear width (the spine less the profile's edge distance on each side, with room for ascent and descent), and the profile's page floor applies; **a generated cover with no layout and no profile named keeps the fixed 14.4 pt type and has no page floor, as it always had** (tested at spines of 0.25 to 0.36 in with the page count known: those digests were taken at the commit before C2). Name a profile to get the edge-aware sizing on a generated cover: on a 0.25 to 0.36 in spine (about 100 to 145 pages of 0.0025 in paper) the fixed type sits within about 2 to 4 pt of the spine's edges. Ingram's 0.03125 in edge for a spine under 0.35 in is in the profile (`SpineEdge`).
- **A wrap** is placed at its measured size and never stretched. Its bleed is its own (`wrap.bleed_in`); an `export.cover` `bleed_in` that disagrees is told (`cover_wrap_bleed`) and the wrap's is used. A measured spine more than 1/32 in from the book's is `cover_wrap_stale`, with both figures and the page count; page 1 is still the picture, so the spine folds fall where the picture has them, and the proof draws where the book's spine would fold, in another colour, with a legend line. A layout made for another trim than the preset's is refused before anything is drawn (`cover_trim_mismatch`, compared with the preset's `page_size`, not its id, so a custom preset works). **The front alone (part `front`) from a wrap** is the front's trim plus the wrap's bleed on its three outer edges and none on the spine side (the page is a bleed narrower than one made of sides): the wrap has no front bleed to give there, and what lies past the front's trim is the spine's art, which a first version drew and a render showed as a dark stripe.
- **The proof** (part `proof`) is the wrap at exactly half size on a page of its own (7 in wide at least) with the trim (red, solid), the edge of the bleed (red, dashed), the spine folds (blue, dashed), the text-safe margin of the profile inside both panels (green, dashed) and the barcode box (orange) drawn over it, and a legend under it with the sizes, the profile and, for a stale wrap, the expected folds. It says it is not for the printer. It is made for every layout, and only for a layout (a cover with no layout has none: `cover_no_proof`). `guides` keeps its meaning for the printer's page (a dark hairline now goes under the white dashes so they show on art).
- **Profiles** (`publish.CoverProfiles`, the one table the PDF and the check read; `coverlayout.Profiles` is built from it): the design's figures, plus the one it left open: **the barcode box's lower right corner is 0.25 in from the back's trim on both sides, for every profile** (the printer's template is authoritative; no printer figure was reconstructed from memory). With no layout the cover keeps its own barcode place (12 per cent of the width, halved) and its guides' 1/8 in safe line unless `profile` is named; the profile's spine rules (the page floor, the edge-aware size) apply only when a profile is named. `compile.cover_spec` reports the rectangle the file draws (a generated cover with no profile named: its own place, and 0.125 in for `text_margin_in`, its guides' line). The barcode box is drawn after the panels, in the profile's size, white, only when `barcode` is on; for a wrap nothing else is drawn.

**Checks.** `export.cover`, `compile.cover_spec` and `project.cover_layout.check` all run `publish.PlanCover`; a test compares the codes of the export and the check for every profile with the barcode on and off. Codes: `image_low_resolution` (under 285 dpi where it prints: print asks for 300, and a picture made at 300 for the trim lands at about 288 once it covers the bleed, which is not worth a warning; the sentence says an image side is scaled up as little as will cover, and a page side or a wrap is never enlarged; one per picture; it replaces C1's screen-size `cover_low_resolution` in the check's output), `cover_print_cropped` (an image side loses over 5 per cent of its picture to fill the panel and its bleed), `cover_margin_crossed` (a page picture inside the profile's text margin of any trim edge), `cover_bleed_missing`, `cover_wrap_stale`, `cover_wrap_bleed`, `cover_barcode_area` (for a wrap or a back with a picture when the profile expects a box and `barcode` is off: an informational note, since art under the box cannot be detected), `spine_narrow` (under a quarter inch, a zero spine included), `spine_few_pages` (the profile's floor), `spine_rtl`, and, from the export only because it reads the picture, `cover_profile_not_srgb` and `cover_profile_dropped`. `check` gains `barcode`, returns the printed dpi in `pictures`, and its `not_checked` is now `colour_profile` (it reads no picture) and, when no `page_count` and `page_thickness_in` are given for sides, `spine_text`. `cover_rtl_text` is a refusal.

**Right-to-left books.** The cover has no running text, so a layout of pictures or a wrap is made: the front goes on the left of the wrap (sides, and a wrap cut by C1's rule), and the spine is left without type (`spine_rtl`). A layout with a generated side is refused in `export.cover` with a sentence (`cover_rtl_text`), because this program sets type left to right in Latin script, as the interior PDF does when it refuses (`ErrRTL`); `compile.cover_spec` and `project.cover_layout.check` still measure it, and carry the same code as a warning. **A project with no layout is untouched by its language:** the generated cover is made as it was on main (type left to right, back on the left), and its measurements work. Because the PDF layout cannot count a right-to-left book's pages, `page_count` must be given for any cover. `compile.cover_spec`'s `front`, `spine` and `back` panels follow the book for a layout (front first for a right-to-left book), and its `barcode_box` is on the back, wherever that is. `cover_page` never meets a right-to-left book (the PDF is refused first).

**Colour.** The PDF stays RGB (DeviceRGB flat fills and untagged pictures, which a printer reads as sRGB; a printer that asks for CMYK converts; the writer has no CMYK path). **A picture's own RGB profile goes into the PDF as its colour space** (`[/ICCBased n 0 R]` with `/Alternate /DeviceRGB`, the profile written once however many pages show the picture): the same rule as the screen derivatives (a valid RGB profile of at most 1 MB whose header states its own size and a class a PDF may name for an image, `mntr`, `scnr` or `spac`; another is left out and `cover_profile_dropped` says so; the tags are not parsed). Before this phase `cleanJPEG` stripped the profile from every picture and the PDF said DeviceRGB (the interior still does, and says `image_colour_profile`), which would have shown a Display P3 cover dull. **No OutputIntent is embedded:** an intent in a file that is not PDF/X or PDF/A claims a conformance this writer does not deliver (no XMP, no trapping key), a RIP that sees one treats the file as that standard, and an sRGB intent would be wrong for a P3 picture beside it; the printer's own conversion of untagged RGB is what DeviceRGB asks for. A non-sRGB profile is reported by `export.cover` (`cover_profile_not_srgb`), not by `check`, which reads no picture. The checker (`internal/publish/check`) accepts an ICCBased colour space only as an RGB profile of three components with an RGB alternate.

**Pictures and memory.** A JPEG goes in as its own stream (`cleanJPEG`'s allow-list copy: the DCT data byte for byte, tested); a PNG that is RGB, grey or indexed, not interlaced and without transparency goes in as its own Flate stream with its predictor. Neither is decoded, resampled or enlarged: every embedded picture has its stored width and height (tested). A PNG with an alpha channel, a transparency chunk, interlacing or 16 bits a channel with alpha is decoded by the writer, which first takes its share of the memory every cover decode shares (`decodeMem`, 600 MB), twice the live figure plus 64 MB: a server that is busy answers 429. Measured through the real path (upload, set, `export.cover`; `TestMeasurePrintCoverMemory`, `SCRIOBH_COVER_RSS`, Apple silicon, `GOMAXPROCS=2`), the Go heap the export alone peaked at, over what it started from:

| Picture | Export's heap peak | Export time |
|---|---|---|
| a 24 MP JPEG wrap (5,772 by 4,158) | 7 MB | 6 ms |
| a 24 MP JPEG front (4,000 by 6,000) | 11 MB | 4 ms |
| a 16 MP RGB PNG wrap, passed through | 17 MB | 56 ms |
| a 16 MP RGBA PNG wrap (decoded) | 230 MB | 480 ms |
| a 16 MP RGBA PNG front (decoded) | 220 MB | 500 ms |
| a 16 MP PNG wrap at 16 bits with alpha (the worst a PNG may be) | 372 MB | 616 ms |

(The 16-bit figure is the heap including garbage the collector has not swept; the process's resident set peaked at 448 MB for it, with the upload and the front's derivative before it, which are C1's costs.) The reservation for that picture is 448 MB, under the 600 MB; the alpha cases are why the figures are large, and why a cover PNG is better sent as a JPEG, as the upload says. A US Letter wrap at 300 dpi (5,325 by 3,375, 18 MP) is a JPEG and is within the cap and passes through.

**`cover_page` (the interior PDF).** `Options.CoverPage` (default off), **for a proof or a copy you print yourself, not for upload to a print-on-demand printer as the interior**: KDP and IngramSpark take the cover as a separate file, and a printer counts and prints every page of the interior, so the two extra pages would be printed and would change the page count and the spine. Page 1 is the front, edge to edge on a page of the book's own size (into the bleed, with the crop marks, when `crop_marks` is on), and page 2 is blank. The blank page is the inside of the cover: with it the title page is still a right-hand page, the mirrored margins still face the right way, a file printed on both sides still has the cover's reverse blank, and a viewer's two-page layout still pairs the book's pages as before. Both are labelled "Cover" (outside the roman and arabic numbering), and the outline points where it did. **They are not interior pages**: `PageCount()`, and with it the spine `compile.cover_spec` computes, does not count them (tested). The front is the layout's (a front picture, or the front cut from a wrap: bleed on its outer edges only, none on the spine side, and no more than the wrap was made with) when `own_cover` is on and the project has one, the generated front otherwise (no layout, a generated front side, or `own_cover` off). A layout made for another trim than the PDF's page size is refused (`cover_trim_mismatch`); `own_cover: false` is the way to use the generated front. The cover page reports the same colour warnings as the print cover (`cover_profile_not_srgb`, `cover_profile_dropped`) as the compile's own, and a low-resolution warning that speaks of the front. A picture that cannot be read refuses the compile (the EPUB falls back to the generated cover; a print file should not change silently). The input hash holds the front's hash (`FrontHash`) only when the layout's front is used, so a project with no layout, a layout with the option off, and `own_cover` off have the hash they had; a change to the back alone is the same file.

**`back_cover` (the EPUB).** `Options.BackCover` (default off): `text/back-cover.xhtml` last in the spine with `linear="no"` (the spine writer had never written the attribute), one `<img>` of the layout's `back` derivative (the back side's picture, or the back cut from a wrap on the side the book's direction puts it), a manifest item `back-cover-image` that is not the `cover-image`, and a link in the navigation document's landmarks (EPUB asks that non-linear content be reachable from the navigation document). It passes `internal/publish/check`, which now reads `linear` and requires a non-linear item to be linked from the navigation document (a test removes the link and the checker names it); **EPUBCheck itself was not run** (not installed here). **The alt text is "Back cover of {title}"**, not the layout's: a layout has one alt text and it describes the cover as the writer wrote it, which for most is the front. Independent of `cover`. The input hash holds the back's hash (`BackHash`: the back side, or the wrap with the side the direction puts the back on), so a change to the front alone is the same file when only the back cover is on. A layout whose back has no picture, no layout, and `own_cover: false` have no back cover page: **a generated back is not put in an ebook**.

**Dry runs and previews.** A dry run has no data directory, so no picture is read: `export.cover` and `export.compile` with `cover_page` still return a file, with a grey box where each picture goes and the warning `image_not_embedded` (the book's own pictures get `image_skipped`), never a failure. The page count a preview shows is the interior's; the file with `cover_page` has two more pages.

**`own_cover` and the release switch.** `own_cover: false` is what a release for a public remote sets (C1). It now also means: the generated front on the PDF's cover page, no back cover page in the EPUB, and the generated wrap from `export.cover`; the layout's pictures and hash are in none of them. `TestOwnCoverOffIsTheGeneratedCoverEverywhere` compiles the EPUB with a back cover, the EPUB, and the PDF with a cover page, with and without a layout, and the bodies and hashes are equal.

**Input hash of the print cover.** What `export.cover` hashed before (the compile hash, the spine, bleed, page count, colour, guides, barcode, title block, project's update time) and, only when a layout is in use, the layout's hash with the book's direction (the layout hash holds the shape, trim, both sides, the pictures' own hashes and the alt text), and, when there is a layout or a `profile` was named, the profile and the page count the spine floor read. The export's manifest records `layout`, `layout_shape`, `profile` and `proof`.

**What the UI work needs.** `export.cover` takes `profile` and `part` (`cover` the default, `front`, `proof`: one file each, named `-cover.pdf`, `-cover-front.pdf`, `-cover-proof.pdf`; the proof of a cover with no layout is refused, `cover_no_proof`); `compile.cover_spec` answers `profile`, `text_margin_in`, `barcode_box` (points from the wrap's left and bottom), `spine_has_text` (now the whole answer) with `spine_text_why` (`narrow`, `few_pages`, `wrap`, `rtl`), and `layout` (shape, trim, `proof`, the wrap's `measured_spine_in`, `stale`, the first page's size, each picture's `dpi` and printed size) when a layout is used; its panels are still the book's own. The warnings above arrive with English messages, codes and, for a cover warning, a `part` (`front`, `back`, `spine`, `wrap`, or empty for the cover as a whole; `compile.Warning.part` is additive and omitted on every other warning; the check's `part` gained `spine`). Refusals of the cover ops carry `data.reason`: `cover_page_count_range`, `cover_thickness_range`, `cover_bleed_range`, `cover_part`, `cover_profile`, `cover_preset_not_print`, `cover_no_trim`, `cover_page_count_needed` (nothing to count: give `page_count`), `cover_page_count_rtl`, `cover_book_too_large`, `cover_plan`, `cover_picture_unusable`, `cover_no_proof`, `cover_trim_mismatch`, `cover_rtl_text`, and `pdf_too_large` (the PDF's word limit, for every PDF compile). Refusals carry `data.reason`: `cover_trim_mismatch` (with `layout_trim` and `page_size`) and `cover_rtl_text`. The options `cover_page` and `back_cover` are in the Compile page's option groups with labels (`cmp.js.o_*`, `cmp.js.h_*`) and are print-only and EPUB-only to the form as the table says. `cmp.cover_note` was rewritten by the UI work below.

**Not verified.** The interior PDF's `cover_page` was never given to a printer or a previewer, and is not meant for one. No print-on-demand previewer (KDP, IngramSpark) was given a produced cover, and no cover was printed: the geometry is the design's figures and the arithmetic is tested to the point, but whether a given printer accepts the file, and whether the barcode inset and the spine type sit where it wants them, is only known by uploading. No CMYK conversion exists. The look was judged from `pdftoppm` renders (poppler), which is not a printer's preflight. The back cover was not opened in a reading system, and EPUBCheck was not run. The proof's legend was read at 110 dpi; its small type was not read on paper.

## As built in C2, the Compile page

`internal/server/compilecover.go`, `web/templates/partials/compile-cover.html`, the `#cmp-cover` section of `web/templates/compile.html`, `compile-print.js` (3.9 KB gzip of its 5 KB, down from 4.8: the drawing moved to the server), `compile.css`. Where it differs from what one would guess:

- **The server measures and draws.** The cover form is an htmx form (`hx-post` to `/ui/projects/{id}/compile/cover-spec`, `hx-sync="this:queue last"`); `compile-print.js` adds the preset and the options on `htmx:configRequest`, says the answer in the status line after the swap, moves focus to a field the answer names (the paper's thickness), and measures again when a cover field, an option or the preset changes (only after a first answer, and only with a thickness). The route calls `compile.cover_spec` and, for a layout, `project.cover_layout.get`, and answers a fragment (200, or 422 with a sentence). The English the ops carry is not shown: warnings are chosen by code, refusals by `data.reason`, in the catalogue (`cmp.cv.w.<code>`, `cmp.cv.r.<reason>`); an unknown code gets a sentence that names it. `TestEveryPrintCodeHasItsWords` reads the back end's source (`pdf_cover_plan.go`, `pdf_cover.go`, `coverlayout/print.go` and `ops.go`, `compile/cover.go`) and fails for a code with no words, or a sentence that leaves a `{placeholder}` open.
- **Warnings carry no `part` and no figures** (`compile.Warning` has only `code` and `message`), so the Compile page builds a sentence from `compile.cover_spec`'s own fields: a low-resolution warning is said once for each picture the layout prints under `publish.LowDPI` (285 dpi), from `layout.pictures`; the stale-wrap sentence has the wrap's measured spine, the page count and the book's spine; the margin sentence has the profile's margin. A margin, bleed or crop warning does not say which side it is about ("A picture on the cover"). Asked of the back end: a `part` on the cover warnings.
- **The drawing** is an SVG in points (the first page's own size: for a wrap, the picture's size and panels, whatever the book's spine is). A wrap is the picture itself (`/files/<attachment>`); sides are the `front_url` and `back_url` derivatives, an image side filling trim plus bleed from the centre and a page side on its background colour, clipped to the panel; a generated side is typeset by a port of the old script's rule (the file sets its own type, so words may break differently). The derivatives are the screen's, so the file crops about 2 per cent tighter; the page says so. Lines are drawn twice (a paper stroke under an ink stroke) and each has its own dash pattern, and the legend's swatches are the same SVG classes, so they cannot drift: the trim, the spine folds, the text margin, the barcode box and, for a stale wrap, where the book's own folds are. Without the layout (or with `own_cover` off) it is the flat cover with the title.
- **Refusals** are sentences with what to do and a link: `cover_trim_mismatch` ("made for 6 × 9 in, this preset is 5 × 8 in ... make it again on the Cover page, or switch off the book's own cover"), `cover_no_proof`, `cover_rtl_text`, a missing or out-of-range thickness. The download route turns the same refusals into the same sentences (`coverPrintError`), so the status line speaks plainly there too.
- **Words for the refusals.** Every `data.reason` the cover ops carry has a sentence here (`cmp.cv.r.*`: the page count, thickness, bleed, part and profile ranges, a preset that isn't print or has no trim, a page count that is needed, can't be worked out for a right-to-left book or a book too long to lay out, `cover_plan`, `cover_picture_unusable`, `pdf_too_large` with its figures, the geometry and picture refusals `cover_trim`, `cover_wrap_missing`, `cover_picture_missing`, `cover_picture_damaged`, `cover_picture_extreme`, `cover_margin_large` and `cover_wrap_*`), so do the form's own (`form_number`, `form_whole`), and a setting of the Options panel that the ops refuse (`options.<key>`, whose sentence names the control by its label; no link to the Cover page, since the cover isn't the cause). A link to the Cover page shows only for a refusal that is about the layout. `TestEveryPrintCodeHasItsWords` reads every `"cover_..."` and `"pdf_..."` literal of the coverlayout and compile packages and fails for one with words neither here nor on the Cover page (the upload's and save's refusals, which a print path cannot meet, use the Cover page's). What is left in English: a refusal that has neither a reason nor a named option (none is met in the tests) shows the op's message. A picture whose file is gone from the server: `compile.cover_spec` does not read files, so it answers and the drawing's `<image>` fails to load, which the script says as an alert; the download is a not-found from the op that the page turns into a sentence. Asked of the back end: have `compile.cover_spec` check that each picture's file exists. A warning's `part` names the side ("The back picture ..."). Once a cover is measured the file for the printer is the solid button and Measure is plain; a refusal puts them back. A refusal is said once, by its alert (the status line is emptied), and focus goes to the field it names only after the person's own Measure, never after a measure that a change elsewhere set off.
- **Three downloads** through `POST .../compile/cover` with `part`: "Download the cover for the printer" (primary), "Front only", "Proof (for you)"; the proof button is hidden for a project with no layout and follows `layout.proof` of each answer. Making the proof a different kind of download is one button and one `part`. `X-Export-Notes` carries (percent-encoded) the sentences for the colour warnings only a file has (a picture's profile that isn't sRGB, or was left out), each naming its picture, once and the manifest lists them.
- **The profile line.** The select shows KDP, IngramSpark and Generic (the default, as in the back end); one line under it gives that profile's text margin, barcode box and the pages from which the spine has type, from `publish.CoverProfiles`, and follows the choice by `:has()` in CSS (without `:has` every line shows). While the cover section is shown the ebook's cover section (`#cmp-coverpage`) is hidden, so there is one link to the Cover page.
- **The Cover page** gained "If you print this cover" (`project.cover_layout.check` against the generic profile at the layout's trim, without a page count: no stale-wrap or spine-text finding), through the same `printWarning`.
- **Not verified:** no printer previewer was given a file; no screen reader; the drawing's generated type was compared by eye with the file's, not measured. The Compile page, like the cover page, is not served offline.

## The cover page as built

`/p/{slug}/cover`, `internal/server/coverui.go`, `web/templates/cover.html`, `web/static/css/cover.css`, `web/static/js/cover.js` (lazy, 3.2 KB gzip of the 5 KB it may have). Where it is not what "The cover page" above says:

- **One form, one button.** The page is a single `multipart/form-data` form (`POST /ui/projects/{id}/cover`) holding the shape, the trim, both sides, the wrap, the bleed and the alt text. Each picture is a plain `<input type=file>` in it. **Save** uploads any file that was chosen (`project.cover_layout.upload`, with the form's trim and bleed) and then sets the layout (`project.cover_layout.set`, built strictly by mode: a generated or left-out side carries nothing, an image only its picture, a page its placement). With script, a file that is chosen or dropped on a drop zone submits the same form at once with a hidden "add the picture" button: the server answers with the draft (the picture added, drawn on its page, nothing saved) so the alt text can be written and Save pressed. The uploaded attachment ids travel in hidden fields, so a refused save never uploads a picture twice. A refusal is a 422 page of the same form with the draft intact (a picture over the one-file limit is a 413 in the same page). A stale `revision` is a 409 page that keeps the writer's version in the form (every typed value and choice, the alt text included, and the pictures already added) and describes the two versions side by side, "Your version" (the form below, as left) and "Saved version" (the other save, with its picture and alt text); the form then carries the revision it met, so Save puts the writer's version in place of theirs, and "Reload the cover" takes theirs and drops the writer's. A picture chosen for a part that the chosen shape does not use (a wrap under "Front and back", a front or a back under the wrap) is not read into the project; the page says so. Under "The generated cover" the first picture in the form decides the shape (the form lists the front, the back, then the wrap). Save also refuses, in the catalogue's sentence, a page placement that the live preview does not draw (a picture more than eight times the page at actual size, or under a pixel), which the layout op alone would accept and the derivative would then fail on. "Use the generated cover" is its own form (`POST .../cover/clear`) with the in-page confirmation of `js/arm.js`.
- **The form's body is read after the caller is authorized, a part at a time.** `uiRoute` is the generic wrapper again (a urlencoded form, `ParseForm`; a multipart body reaches no route that uses it). The cover form's route is registered with `uiRouteStream` (`handlers.go`), which does, in this order: the session; the project and the role (`project.get`, an editor; a non-member gets 404, a reader 403), reading no body; the CSRF token, from the `X-CSRF-Token` header that `cover.js` and `checkCSRF` honour, else as the form's first field `_csrf` (read with a 256-byte limit; a form whose token comes later is refused); a place: one cover body at a time for each person (a second, from the same account in another tab or on another connection, is 429 at once with `cover.err.busy_you`) and at most two bodies in all (`bodySlotCount`), so that one person cannot hold every place; otherwise 429 with `Retry-After` and the page's sentence (`cover.err.busy_form`); the body's pace (below); and only then the body, through `r.MultipartReader()`. A field is read to at most 64 KB; a picture is handed to `project.cover_layout.upload` as a stream the moment it arrives, so the whole body is never parsed or held (the op holds the one picture it is checking, up to the upload limit) and no part goes to a temporary file. The shape and the trim must come before any picture and the bleed before the wrap (the page's form is in that order; a client that sends them after is told so). After a picture is refused the rest of the form is still read, so the answer shows the draft whole. `bodyLimit` gives a multipart cover post three times the upload limit and a megabyte (an urlencoded one the default); past it the page answers 413 in `cover.err.body_large`. The route is in the gate sweep and the route scan (`uiRouteStream` is scanned like `uiRoute`), and `TestGateStreamRoutesAuthorizeBeforeTheBody` sends the multipart body the urlencoded sweeps never reach: an outsider is 404, a reader 403, an editor of a locked project the unlock page, each with no byte of the body read, and the source scan fails a `uiRouteStream` whose authorize function is nil or not a real function.

  Measured with `TestMeasureCoverBodyMemory` (a real listener, a 74 MB multipart body generated as it is sent, the Go heap sampled every 2 ms, `GOMAXPROCS=2`, Apple silicon; set `SCRIOBH_MEASURE_COVER_BODY=1`):

  | Scenario (each body 74 MB) | Whole body parsed first (before) | Streamed after authorizing (now) |
  |---|---|---|
  | a signed-in non-member posts one | heap +223 to +257 MB (3.0 to 3.5 times the body), 404 | +0 MB, 404 |
  | eight non-members at once | +1,290 to +1,350 MB, all 404 | +8 to +9 MB, all 404 |
  | a member posts one (a 74 MB junk file) | +225 MB, 422 | +53 MB, 413 (the op reads one picture up to the 25 MB limit and refuses it) |
  | eight members at once | +1,230 to +1,510 MB | +116 MB: two bodies read (413), six told 429 |

    **The pace of a body.** `http.Server`'s `ReadTimeout` (60 s) and `WriteTimeout` (60 s) are absolute: left alone they would cut a writer on a slow uplink off in the middle of a 25 MB picture, and would let two connections that send a byte a second hold both places until they ran out (reproduced, and repeatable by reconnecting every minute). For this route the body is wrapped (`pacedBody`) and each `Read` is given a read deadline of `bodyIdle` (15 s) from the moment it starts (through `http.NewResponseController`), so the limit counts only the time spent waiting for the sender and never the time the server spends working on what it has already read, such as cleaning the front picture on a slow ARMv7 or riscv64 machine before the back one is read (`TestIdleLimitDoesNotCountTheServersOwnWork`: a handler that reads 1 MiB, works for three times the idle limit and reads on got "read 0 then i/o timeout" before this was changed): a connection that sends nothing for 15 s is dropped and its places are freed, while a slow one is received for as long as bytes keep coming, up to `bodyCeiling` (15 minutes in all, then the read fails). **The floor.** A sender who dribbles a byte every 14 s is never idle for 15 s, so the idle limit alone would let two accounts hold both places for the whole 15 minutes. So, once a body has spent `bodyGrace` (30 s) waiting inside `Read`, its average rate over the time spent waiting must be at least `bodyFloor` (32 KiB/s); below that the read fails (`errTooSlow`), the connection is dropped and the places are freed. Why 32 KiB/s: a picture of the default largest size (`SCRIOBH_UPLOAD_MAX_MB`, 25 MiB) must be able to finish inside the 15-minute ceiling on the slowest uplink we accept, and 25 MiB in 900 s is 28.4 KiB/s; 32 KiB/s (about 0.26 Mbit/s) takes 13.3 minutes and leaves some margin. An uplink slower than the floor could not have finished a largest picture inside the ceiling anyway; it can still send a small one (it is dropped only after the grace, and only if it stays that slow). The rate is measured over the time spent waiting for the sender, like the idle limit, so the server's own work between reads never counts against a client; a body that arrives in a burst is judged by its average, so a sender who front-loads bytes buys time in proportion to what they sent. Tests (`TestATricklingBodyIsDroppedBelowTheFloor`: grace and floor shortened, a byte every 400 ms, dropped within seconds and the place free again; the steady-uplink test above is unaffected). The write deadline is lifted to the ceiling plus a minute so the answer can still be written; when the body is whole the read deadline is cleared, so it cannot cancel the request while the picture is cleaned. Tests with a real `http.Server` and the production timeouts: `TestOnePersonCannotHoldTheCoverBodySlots` (one person trickles a byte a second: their second body is 429, two other editors are admitted, and when the trickler goes quiet it is dropped after about 15 s and their place is free) and `TestASlowUplinkIsNotCutByTheAbsoluteTimeout` (timeouts shortened to 2 s; a 5 s upload that keeps sending is received and answered).

  A test (`TestCoverFormReadsNoBodyBeforeItAuthorizes`) shows that for a non-member, a reader and a wrong token the server has taken under 8 MB of a 60 MB body when it says no.
- **Which controls show follows what is chosen,** by `:has(:checked)` in CSS, with no script; a browser without `:has` shows every control, which still works. The nine positions are a native radio grid (Tab to the group, arrows inside it; with script ArrowUp and ArrowDown also move a row). The backgrounds are the same eight swatches as the new-project form.
- **The preview is not CSS `object-fit`.** The plan said page mode would be previewed live with `object-fit` and `object-position`. They cannot say a scale below 100, an actual size or a margin, and the page's CSP and the audit allow no `style` attribute, which is what a continuous value would need. So the front of a draft is an inline SVG whose `<image>` and clip rectangle carry the numbers, placed by `coverlayout.PlacePage` on the server and by a line-for-line port of it (same rounding, and the rule that a picture is never enlarged) in `cover.js`; when the back end changed the placing to stop enlarging, the e2e comparison failed until the port followed. `scripts/e2e-covers.mjs` checks that the live drawing and the server's drawing of the same draft have the same attributes, for fit inside, fill and crop, and actual size. A saved layout is shown by its own derivative JPEG, at the library tile's size and at a page's size, and the back when it has a picture.
- **The wrap's drawing** is one SVG: the picture, the cut line, the two spine folds and the 0.25 in text margin (the generic profile's; the printer's own figure is for the print cover), each drawn twice (a paper-coloured stroke under an ink-coloured one) so it shows on any picture. The measured resolution, the spine in inches and in pages of KDP white paper (0.002252 in a page, stated) and the side the front is on are listed beside it, from `measured` when the layout is saved and from `coverlayout.MeasureWrap` for a picture just chosen.
- **Words.** Every sentence is a `cover.*` key. A warning is chosen by its `code`, with the figures from the layout's `pictures` (dpi, share cropped); a refusal by `data.reason`. The English the ops carry is never shown. A code the page does not know gets a general sentence that names it. Every code the back end can give (every `"cover_..."` string literal of its source, however it reaches a response) has its sentence, and a test fails when one is added without; the wrap's own measure has its own low-resolution sentence (it speaks of the wrap's dpi at the trim with its bleed, not of a picture placed on a page), and the codes `MeasureWrap` refuses with, with `cover_wrap_invalid`, have their warning sentences. The kind of book on the generated cover comes from the catalogue (`kind.*`) on this page; the library tile and the Continue line use an older shared partial (`kind-label`) on a page that is not in the catalogue yet, so they still say it in English: that includes the progressive-JPEG refusal (`cover_picture_progressive`), the small-picture warning of page mode (`cover_picture_small`) and the colour-profile warning (`cover_profile_not_srgb`). After a clear the form sends revision 0, which the op accepts.
- **The tree's Cover row** is a link above the tree, outside `role="tree"` and not a `.tree-row`, so the tree's keyboard model, its counts ("Manuscript, N documents"), the jump list, drag and drop and the row menu never meet it: it is its own Tab stop before the tree, and a click on it goes through the writing view's `W.go` so the page is left only when the text is saved. It cannot be moved, trashed or excluded because it is not a document. In a project with no documents it is still there, above the empty-tree sentence.
- **Reload with the network cut.** The shell's cache (`/static/` and the offline page) holds no project page and no picture, so the cover page, like every project page, is not served offline: a reload shows the offline notice and nothing of the cover. `e2e-covers` checks that, and that the cache holds nothing of the cover.
- **Entry points:** the tree row, the "Cover" entry of the project's menu (`project-more`, and the library's project dialog), a section of the Compile page that is always visible (the print section is hidden until a print preset is chosen; the print integration is C2), and the EPUB import's "Use this picture as the project's cover" (`import-books.js`, only for a new project and only when `can_use_as_cover`; `use_reason` is shown as the back end gives it).
- **The library tile** keeps its 3:4 box, fits the picture inside it on the cover colour, and its `alt` is "Cover: {description}" from the catalogue, because the link's name begins with the picture.

## Decisions

Taken by the owner on 17 November 2026, each as recommended: (1) project data with a fixed Cover row in the tree; (2) a page side is a picture placed on a page, with no text side; (3) one layout per project; (4) `golang.org/x/image/draw` for scaling; (5) KDP and IngramSpark paperback profiles, plus generic. The reasoning for each is kept below.


1. **Cover rows or cover documents.** Recommended: the layout is project data with a fixed Cover row in the tree (above). The alternative follows Vellum and Atticus: cover pages are documents in the tree. That need not rebuild `documents`: a `front_matter` or `back_matter` document with a new `cover_role` column (`front`, `back`, `wrap`) and its picture as content would do. It makes a cover something the writer moves, compiles and trashes like any page, and a picture then carries a document's machinery (revisions, word counts, the editor), and the screen surfaces (library, site, `project.cover`) must find the cover by searching the tree.
2. **"Standard page" means a picture placed on a page.** That is what this design builds. The other reading, an ordinary text page (a back-cover blurb typeset as a page, as Pressbooks' "About" text and KDP Cover Creator's back description do), is not in this design. It could be a third side mode, `text`, using the typesetter and a document from the manuscript as its source.
3. **One layout, or one per format.** This design has one layout per project (the ebook takes its front). Some authors use a different ebook cover from the print front. A per-format override (`ebook_front`) is a small addition if wanted.
4. **The scaler.** `golang.org/x/image/draw` (recommended) or a hand-written filter.
5. **Print profiles.** KDP and IngramSpark paperback to start; Lulu, BookBaby and others later only from their published figures.
